The quarries for this lime were none other than the outermost edge of the shore, often a block under the very water, where the limestone was won with crowbar and hammer, without effort. When the chips were separated from each other, darts or island nails lay as close together as chaff in coarse bread, that God only knows where so many rare shells had been taken.

A petrification, rare in other places, but common here, resembled a valvulam echini, often as large as the flat of the hand, or else looked like a crescent with 2 parallel furrows and several transverse streaks.
