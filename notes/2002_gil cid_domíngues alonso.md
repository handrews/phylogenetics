SYSTEMATIC PALEONTOLOGY

Class EOCRINOIDEA Jaekel, 1918

(Nodal Group of Pelmatozoa)

Genus Ubaghsicystis gen. nov.

Etymology: The genus is dedicated to Professor Ubaghs, in recognition of his extensive work on the study of Paleozoic echinoderms, as well as for his continuous and cordial opinions regarding Spanish Paleozoic echinoderms.

Type species: Ubaghsicystis nov. sp.

Diagnosis: eocrinoid with a globose theca formed by approximately 40 plates. Epispirals present in the upper half of the theca and absent in the lower half. Anal pyramid in a lateral position above the equatorial line of the theca. Long, homeomorphic, holomeritus peduncle with a distal fixation disc. Columnars: circular in cross-section, narrow lumen, and without articular crenulations. Thin, biserial arms with covering plates and without brachioles.

Ubaghsicystis segurae nov. gen. et sp.

Attributed species: monospecific genus, Ubaghsicystis seguarae nov. sp.

Description: The specimen appears very complete with brachioles, theca, peduncle, and attachment in anatomical connection; the fossil is preserved as a natural mold in a greenish sandy shale. On the surface of the mold, there are deposits of iron oxide in ochre-yellow tones with a powdery appearance. The fossil is divided into two slabs of shale, which we will call the "part" (Figure 3.1-2: Figure 4) and the "counterpart" (Figure 3.4-6). The "part" has two brachioles approximately 7 mm long and 0.2-0.3 mm in cross-section; a theca with an apparent diameter of 5.5 mm (the toca is collapsed) and a height (oral-aborol) of 5.5 mm; in the "part," a portion of the podunityle, 7 mm long, is preserved. In the "conírapanto" is the peduncle along its entire length (0 mm) with a discoidal attachment of 1.7 mm in diameter; at the opposite end is a basal portion of the locus approximately 4.5 mm long and 2 mm wide.

Based on the diagram, we assign to each plate two numbers separated by a period: the first indicates the cycle to which the plate belongs (from 1 to 5) and the second the position of the plate within the cycle (from 1 to 12). Since the sp. presents a pattern of plate distribution that tends towards irregularity in the proximal areas of the oral region, we find an increasing difficulty in identifying cycles 4, 5, 6, and 7 (Figure 5).

Theca - The loca has an ovoid shape and is taller than it is wide. The transition from the theca to the peduncle is oblique, while that of the bracts is quite progressive (Figure 4.2: 4.8). The local plates are generally 6-7 sides and their external surface is smooth, convex, and sometimes with a central indentation (Figure 4.1). The general external surface of the loca is curved.

In the lower half of the theca, the plates have a regular arrangement in cycles, which becomes irregular in the upper half (Figure 4.2). The plates of the inner half are large and have an almost regular outline, while those of the upper half are small and have a less regular outline, some of them with a concave edge (Figure 5).

At the base of the rock, a basal cycle is clearly distinguishable, formed by three plates that are both very thin and tall (Figure 5, plates 1.1, 1.2, and 1.3). This cycle represents a small part of the total height of the theca. The upper portion of the plates is thin, but towards the base they become thicker, perhaps as a mechanical reinforcement for the insertion of the peduncle. The lateral sutures between the basal plates are rectilinear. The external surface of this cycle is plano-convex.

Above the basal cycle, there is a cycle of plates that we will call, without intending to establish relationships with other polymalozoans, the inferior cycle (Figure 5, plates 2.1-6). This cycle consists of fewer plates and is the largest in the theca. Its external surface is smooth, convex, and with a controlled interior. One of the plates (23) presents a sizable ridge with an asymmetrical profile and whose upper edge is abruptly oriented towards the oral region. Some latex molds present a small, elongated, omega-shaped pore (W). This pore may be an artifact, but if not, it could be the isidroporo-gonopore (Figure 3.3). Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) examination has not been able to confirm this. The sutures of these plates are rosy. Some epispirals appear on the upper edge of these plates. Above the infralateral plates, we have a third cycle of lateral plates (Figures 31-7). The lateral cycle is formed by at least two plates with an irregular polygonal outline, with the exception of plate 3.2, which is hexagonal. This plate has a flat surface, an abomisate with a central dome. The remaining plates are curved but without any distinguishable central structures. The fourth and fifth cycles are irregular, and the attribution of the plates in this area to one cycle or the other cannot be made without a certain degree of arbitrariness. The size of the plates of cycles three to five decreases progressively towards the oral zone; the outline of these plates becomes irregular, with some of them becoming almost triangular (e.g., plates 4.4, 4.5, 5.4). The anal zone is conspicuous, situated above the equatorial line of the theca between cycles three and five, and is slightly displaced with respect to the pirible sidroporo-gonopore. The periproct is a pyramid formed by spiniform inongular ossicles, very elongated (five times longer than incised). Nine ossicles are observed, but their number must have been, by extrapolation, around fifteen. The anal pyramid is as long as it is incised and, due to the position of the ossicles, is bilateral in the oral- The pyramid is articulated on a periprocl window of circular outline; the apex (anus) is projected towards the outer portions of the pyramid. The periprocl window is formed by irregular tecol plates between which ossicles of triangular outline are inserted (plates 4.7 and 5.7).

Epispires - The epispires are simple, with a rounded outline and no appreciable dilutions between their diameters (Figures 4.2, 4.4, 4.8). It is not possible to observe covering structures. The epispires are found in pairs or other groups, although between plates 2.3 and 2.5 (Figure 4.5) we find two confluent epispires, whose external appearance must have been that of a single epispire. Twenty-seven epispires are observed, although we estimate that their total number must have been somewhat less than 50. Each epispire is normally located at the suture of two plates, although there are some cases of epispires located at the vertex of three plates (for example, between plates 2.2, 3.2 and 3.3 or between plates 3.2, 4.2 and 4.3). In other cases, there are two epispirals per suhua (for example, between plates 3.3 and 4.3). Regarding their structure, they have an externally raised, semicircular rim that stands out from the general surface of the plates. The outer edge of these structures is oblique, and the inner edge is abrupt, almost perpendicular to the plate surface. The bottom of the epispirals is concave, and the thecal pore opens within it (Figures 4.4 and 4.5). The epispirals are located in the upper half of the theca, in the areas above the contact edge between the second and third cycles. There is a symmatous zone near the periproct without epispirals, possibly due to the proximity of the digestive bacillus (Figure 4.8).

Oral region - There is no evidence of the oral surface, nor of the total number of arms. In the sample, we have two almost complete articulated arms (Figure 3.1). Each arm is biserial, with the plates on one side alternating with those on the other, and a cover plate directly over each of them (Figure 4.8). The brachiolar plates are two to three times taller than they are wide, and the cover plates are three to four times taller than they are wide. The arms are as long as the theca is tall and must have been flexible, at least orally, since both retain a certain curvature of their apices towards the mouth. In the basal portions where they meet the theca, the plates are much more robust, especially the cover plates. In fact, plates 6.1 and 6.2 could be local s.str. plates, brachiolar plates, or oral plates.

Peduncle - The peduncle is complete from its insertion into the theca (columnar 1) to the distal attachment disc ("holdlásí") and is almost twice the height of the theca (7.8 mm). It is holomeric, thin, with a circular cross-section (between 0.8 mm and 0.5 mm in diameter). There are 33 columnar plates. Although the peduncle is somewhatomorphic, it is possible to distinguish several regions. In the proximal region, there are five plates with a rounded cross-section, though not milliform, very low (axial; 0.1 mm high) and slightly wider (transverse) than the rest (between 0.8 mm wide for the first and 0.6 mm for the fifth). Following this, there is an increase in height (columnars 6 to 10). The next region is very uniform with cylindrical plates, almost as high as they are wide. Finally, the last 6 plates (columnars 28 to 33) can be distinguished; their lateral surface is convex, forming a highly flexible zone with the attachment disc. In fact, the specimen shows an 1100 mm flexure in this region.
The distal attachment disc is convex with a small central pit where the distal plate separates from the peduncle (columnar 33). The disc has an almost circular contour with a diameter of 1.4–1.6 mm and a height of 0.4 mm at its center and 0.8 mm at the periphery.
Relationships and differences: Ubaghsicystis segurae nov. gen. et sp. is clearly distinguished from the other described species of eocrinoides, being the only one known with a solomeric peduncle longer than the theca and with opiaspires located in the upper half of a theca formed by large plates.
