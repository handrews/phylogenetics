# Review: Whiteaves 1897 — "Description of a New Genus and Species of
Cystideans from the Trenton Limestone at Ottawa" and its 1897/98 postscript

No source record and no tree exist for this paper (`1897_whiteaves` is not
in `sources.yaml`; confirmed by `review_1962_fay.md`, which already notes
this). Per the brief, this review summarises what the paper prints so the
owner can decide whether to enter it, rather than checking a tree against
the text.

Text: `1897_whiteaves.txt` (OCR of *The Canadian Record of Science*, vol.
VII). Two passages are in scope: the protologue (assigned printed pp.
287–292) and the postscript renaming the genus (assigned printed p. 395,
continuing to 396).

## Page mapping

Printed folios are read directly off the scan, not derived from a single
volume-wide offset — the volume is a bound run of quarterly numbers, each
carrying its own unpaginated front matter (Natural History Society officer
lists, subscription notices, blank leaves), so the PDF-index-to-page offset
resets at each number boundary and cannot be extrapolated across them.

- **Protologue.** PDF index 358–363 carry printed folios 287–292 directly
  ("287" top of index 358 … "292" top of index 363; offset index − 71 holds
  for this whole stretch). The section opens mid-Number: index 338 carries
  the Number's own title page, "VOL. VII. JANUARY, 1897. No. 5." — so the
  protologue is in **Number 5**.
- **Postscript.** PDF index 490 is Number 7's cover/contents page ("VOLUME
  VII, NUMBER 7 … CONTENTS … Postscript to a "Description of a New Genus and
  Species of Cystideans …" By J. F. Whiteaves … 395"); index 493 carries the
  visible folio "396" at its top. Index 492 (the postscript's own opening,
  between the two) is therefore printed **p. 395**, though the folio itself
  is not legible on that scan — the run 490→493 has no other candidate page.
  Index 492's masthead line reads (OCR) "VOL. VII. UY 5. 1897. Noa," —
  **cannot verify** the exact characters, but "UY 5." most plausibly
  represents "JULY" and "Noa," represents "No. 7." (Numbers 5, 6 and 8 are
  headed "JANUARY, 1897", "APRIL, 1897" and "OCTOBER, 1897" respectively —
  index 338, 414, 566 — a quarterly Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct pattern that leaves July
  for No. 7).

**Issue dates versus nominal cover dates (bears directly on the naming
history — see Cases).** Each Number's back cover carries an "ISSUED …"
notice for the Number just concluded, immediately before the next Number's
title page:

| Number | title-page date | back-cover "ISSUED" notice (= its own actual issue date, per the notice preceding the *next* Number) |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | "JANUARY, 1897" (index 338) | "ISSUED 12TH JUNE, 1897" (line 38746, before No. 6's title at 39028) |
| 6 | "APRIL, 1897" (index 414) | "ISSUED 27TH OCTOBBR [October], 1897" (line 45821, before No. 7's contents at 45833) |
| 7 | "[JULY?], 1897" (index 492) | "ISSUED 7TH JANUARY, 1898" (line 51420, before No. 8's title at 51432) |
| 8 | "OCTOBER, 1897" (index 566) | — |

So Number 5 (protologue, cover-dated January 1897) was actually issued 12
June 1897, and Number 7 (postscript, cover-dated apparently July 1897) was
actually issued **7 January 1898**. This is independently confirmed by
Bather himself: his 1914 paper states the wrapper date "January, 1897… is
obviously erroneous, since the author dated his own contribution 'April
28th, 1897', and copies of it were first received in London on July 31 of
that year" (p. 193), and separately cites "a 'Postscript' (tom. cit., p.
395, **January 7, 1898**)" (p. 193) — matching this review's date derived
independently from the volume's own issue notices.

## (a) Front matter

Both pieces are in the same serial, no existing `publications.yaml` entry:
searched for "Canadian Record" / "Record of Science" — **no match**; a
journal record would need to be created.

- **Masthead (both pieces):** "THE CANADIAN RECORD OF SCIENCE. INCLUDING
  THE PROCEEDINGS OF THE NATURAL HISTORY SOCIETY OF MONTREAL, AND REPLACING
  THE CANADIAN NATURALIST." Volume title page (index 4): "VOL. VII.
  (1896–1897.) … MONTREAL: PUBLISHED BY THE NATURAL HISTORY SOCIETY. 1897."
- **Protologue.** "VOL. VII. JANUARY, 1897. No. 5" (nominal; actually issued
  12 June 1897 — see above). Pages 287–292. Heading: "DESCRIPTION OF A NEW
  GENUS AND SPECIES OF CYSTIDEANS FROM THE TRENTON LIMESTONE AT OTTAWA."
  with a footnote "¹ Communicated by permission of the Director of the
  Geological Survey." Byline: "By J. F. Whiteaves." Closing line (p. 292):
  "Orrawa [Ottawa], April 28th, 1897." — the author's own composition date.
- **Postscript.** "VOL. VII. [JULY?], 1897. No. 7" (nominal, OCR-uncertain
  month; actually issued 7 January 1898 — see above). Page 395 (–396).
  Heading: "POSTSCRIPT TO A 'DESCRIPTION OF A NEW GENUS AND SPECIES OF
  CYSTIDEANS FROM THE TRENTON LIMESTONE AT OTTAWA.'" Byline: "By J. F.
  Whiteaves." The Number's own contents page credits the byline as "By J.
  F. Whiteaves, F.R.S.C." — the article's own byline omits "F.R.S.C." Closing
  line: "OTTAWA. Sept. 14th, 1897." — the author's own composition date,
  nearly four months before the piece actually appeared in print.

Whether the postscript should be one source record with the protologue or
its own (different Number, composed ~5 months later, issued ~7 months
after that) is a modelling question this review does not resolve; A13's
"collection of several works" and A11's "one work, several printings" are
both close but not exact fits — this is one *serial*, two separate short
notes, roughly comparable to Hall's Additions and Corrections case (G7)
but crossing a year boundary.

## (b) Names and nomenclatural acts

**1. *Astrocystites* (gen. nov.) and *Astrocystites ottawaensis* (sp.
nov.), p. 287.** Heading (combining genus and species with no separate
diagnosis or "n. gen., n. sp." notation): "ASTROCYSTITES OTTAWAENSIS."
Description runs directly from the heading with no formal break between
genus- and species-level statements — this is a single continuous
description, not a genus diagnosis followed by a species description. No
type-species phrase is ever printed (searched the whole protologue; matches
the pattern already noted for Fay 1962 in `review_1962_fay.md`, extended
here to the original description itself); the genus is monotypic in this
paper, so any type fixation is by original monotypy in the modern sense,
never stated as such.

**2. Postscript renaming act, p. 395–396, quoted in full (OCR as printed,
one Greek word not legible):**

> "In the January number of this journal for the current year the writer
> endeavoured to describe a new genus and species of blastoid-like
> cystideans from the Trenton limestone at Ottawa, under the name
> *Astrocystites Canadensis*. Since this description was published, the
> writer has been informed by Mr. F. A. Bather, M.A., of the Natural
> History Department of the British Museum, that Haeckel in 1896 separated
> *Asteroblastus tuberculatus*, Schmidt, from the typical *A. stellatus* of
> Eichwald, under the new generic name *Asterocystis*. Mr. Bather thinks
> that there is not sufficient difference between the words *Astrocystites*
> and *Astrocystis*, and it would obviously be inconvenient to use such
> essentially similar names for two such closely related genera. The
> writer, therefore, desires to be allowed to substitute the generic name
> *Steganoblastus* (from [Greek, illegible in this scan]: Closely covered,
> with reference to the large covering plates and covered mouth, as kindly
> suggested by Mr. Bather) for that of *Astrocystites*, which is practically
> preoccupied, and to retain the original specific name. In other words it
> is desired that the genus and species in future be called *Steganoblastus
> Canadensis* instead of *Astrocystites Canadensis*." (pp. 395–396)

No word for the act itself ("nom. subst.", "nom. nov.") is printed; it is
stated as a request/desire to substitute a preoccupied name. The species
epithet is not touched ("to retain the original specific name") — but both
sentences of the postscript that name the combination in full say
"*Canadensis*", not *ottawaensis*. This is **not an OCR artifact**: Bather's
1914 paper (p. 193) independently confirms the printed text and calls it
Whiteaves' own slip — "In this Postscript he inadvertently alluded to the
species as *Steganoblastus canadensis* instead of *S. ottawaensis*." So the
postscript, read in isolation, misnames its own species twice.

**The objection and its ground, exactly as printed.** Bather (via
Whiteaves' report of his letter) objects on grounds of confusability with
*Asterocystis* Haeckel, 1896 — not *Asterocystites* and not (as the task's
framing put it) an objection grounded in *Asteroblastus* itself. Haeckel's
genus is independently confirmed as *Asterocystis* (not the second
occurrence's OCR spelling "Astrocystis") by `review_1896_haeckel.md` §(b):
"Genus: *Asterocystis*, E. Haeckel (nov. gen.)." (Haeckel 1896, p. 116),
raised to separate Schmidt's *Asteroblastus tuberculatus* from Eichwald's
*A. stellatus* — matching the postscript's own account exactly. Within the
postscript's four sentences the spelling wobbles between "*Asterocystis*"
(first mention) and "*Astrocystis*" (second, four lines later) — **cannot
verify** which the original type set, though the independent Haeckel-review
confirmation favours "*Asterocystis*" as correct both times.

## (c) Material

**Protologue (p. 290–291):** "Two specimens of this species, both collected
by Mr. John Stewart in 1886 from the Trenton limestone at Division street,
Ottawa, are in the Museum of the Geological Survey of Canada, and an
imperfect specimen from the same locality has been kindly lent to the
writer by Mr. Walter R. Billings." Three specimens total; no catalogue
number is given for any of them, and no role word ("type", "holotype",
"syntype") is used anywhere in the protologue or the postscript — searched
exhaustively, zero hits for "type" in either piece. This matches the
Ground rules' expectation for an 1897 note: "no catalogue number… a name
proposed in a sentence."

The postscript adds no material.

**Later re-description of this same material (for cross-reference only —
not this source's own text).** Bather 1914 (pp. 193–194) retrospectively
labels the three as "the three syntypes of Whiteaves," distinguishes them
A/B/C, and states A and B (Stewart's, ex-GSC) are "in the Victoria Memorial
Museum, Ottawa" while C (the Billings loan) "remains" in Billings' own
possession as of 1914 — and **selects A as "the holotype."** Fay 1962 (per
`review_1962_fay.md`) instead describes only **two** syntypes, one bearing
GSC No. 752 (which Fay calls "the holotype" in a plate caption while the
body text calls it "labelled a syntype"), the other "lent to Mr. Hudson…
[which] disappeared" when Hudson died. Whiteaves' own 1897 text supports
neither later count directly: it names three specimens (two GSC, one
Billings), no Hudson loan, no catalogue number. The Hudson loan therefore
post-dates 1914 (Bather does not mention it) and must refer to a
subsequent loan of one of Bather's three, not a fourth specimen — **cannot
verify** which of A/B/C went to Hudson, or when. This three-way
correspondence problem (Whiteaves 1897 → Bather 1914 → Fay 1962) is a
genuine cross-source discrepancy in specimen count and type designation
that this dataset will need to resolve when a tree is built, not something
this review can settle from the 1897 text alone.

## (d) Locality and horizon

"Trenton limestone at Division street, Ottawa" (p. 290, protologue) is the
only locality/horizon statement in either piece; no more specific horizon,
member, or bed is given. (Bather 1914, p. 194, independently confirms: "The
precise horizon has nowhere been stated.")

## (e) Illustrations

Protologue only, p. 287 (three text-figures, unnumbered plate — printed at
the head of the article before the description, drawn by L. M. Lambe per
the closing acknowledgement, p. 292): "Fig. 1. Side view of a nearly
perfect specimen… Fig. 2. Summit view of the same specimen… Fig. 3. Radial
plate on the left of the anal region of another specimen, twice the
natural size…" No illustrations accompany the postscript.

## (f) Classification and earlier work cited

No formal class/order/family scaffold is printed anywhere in either piece
— this is a short descriptive note, not a systematic chapter, and
classification is argued entirely in prose:

- "*Astrocystites* would seem to be most nearly related to *Asteroblastus*,
  Eichwald, and is probably referable to the same family, though it
  clearly differs from that genus in several important particulars" (p.
  290), followed by a detailed plate-by-plate comparison of the two
  genera's calyx and summit structure (pp. 290–291).
- Comparison to *Blastoidocrinus* (E. Billings, Chazy limestone of the
  Island of Montreal, and "Orthoceratitenkalk" of Pulkowa, Russia,
  described by Friedrich Schmidt) and to *Cystoblastus*, Volborth, "in
  Zittel's description of the latter genus, which is the only one that the
  writer has access to" (p. 291) — an explicit statement of secondhand,
  single-source access to a taxon under comparison.
- Schmidt (1874, cited in a footnote as "Mémoires de l'Académie Impériale
  des Sciences de St.-Pétersbourg, VIIe Série, tome XXI, p. 25") is cited
  for the opinion that *Blastoidocrinus*, *Asteroblastus*, *Mesites* and
  *Cystoblastus* are Cystidea intermediate in character to the Blastoids.
- Charles Eastman's 1896 translation of Zittel's *Text-book of
  Palaeontology* vol. 1 is quoted verbatim on whether such Ordovician forms
  might eventually be moved to the Blastoidea (p. 292).
- Etheridge & Carpenter's *Catalogue of the Blastoidea in the Geological
  Department of the British Museum* (1886), p. 129, is quoted rejecting
  *Blastoidocrinus* from true Blastoidea (p. 292).
- Nicholson & Lydekker's *Manual of Palaeontology* vol. 1 (1889) is cited
  for adopting "Johannes Muller's classification of the Cystoidea," which
  divides that class "into the three orders of the Aporitidæ, Diploporitidæ
  and Rhombiferi" (OCR renders these "Aporitide," "Diploporitidee,"
  "Rhombiferi" — **cannot verify** the exact ligature/diacritic forms
  printed); Whiteaves tentatively refers *Astrocystites* to "the
  Diploporitidæ" (p. 292) on that scheme — his only concrete, if
  provisional, order-level placement anywhere in the piece.
- The postscript cites only Haeckel (1896) for *Asterocystis*, as quoted
  above (§b).

No order, family or class is ever *erected* here; every classificatory
statement is comparative and hedged ("would seem to be," "probably
referable," "would seem to be most probably referable").

## (g) Uncertainties

- Postscript's masthead month ("UY 5. 1897.") — **cannot verify** the exact
  reading; inferred as "JULY" from the quarterly Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct pattern of
  the volume's other three Numbers checked (5, 6, 8).
- Postscript's Greek etymology for *Steganoblastus* — illegible OCR
  ("oreyavoc"); Bather's 1914 reprint of the same etymology (also OCR'd,
  "ateyavos") is likewise not legible as Greek script; both almost
  certainly render στεγανός ("covered") — **cannot verify** the exact
  printed characters from either scan.
- The spelling of Haeckel's genus wobbles between "*Asterocystis*" and
  "*Astrocystis*" within four lines of the postscript (§b) — **cannot
  verify** which, if either, is an OCR error versus a printer's error; an
  independent source review (`review_1896_haeckel.md`) supports
  "*Asterocystis*" as Haeckel's actual spelling.
- "Aporitidæ," "Diploporitidæ" spellings (§f) — **cannot verify** exact
  diacritics/ligatures through this OCR.
- `taxa.yaml`'s `steganoblastus` record carries `auth: [whiteaves], year:
  1899`. The authorship credit matches this review (Whiteaves published
  the name; Bather only suggested it, and is credited as such in the
  text). The **year does not**: nothing in either the protologue, the
  postscript, or Bather's 1914 account supports 1899 — the postscript is
  composed 14 September 1897 and, on both this review's own reconstruction
  of the volume's issue notices and Bather's independent 1914 statement, was
  actually published 7 January 1898. This looks like a data-entry mixup
  with the unrelated `1899_bather` source (a different paper, already in
  this dataset) rather than anything printed here.
- Whether the *Steganoblastus* replacement act is best modelled as part of
  the same source record as the protologue, or as its own record — see
  §(a) — is left open.
- The specimen count/type-designation discrepancy across Whiteaves 1897,
  Bather 1914 and Fay 1962 (§c) is reported, not resolved.
