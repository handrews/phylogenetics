# Audit: 1854c_billings (Billings, "Cystidea from the Trenton Limestone, Second Paper," Canadian Journal 2:268–274)

## 1. Page-index mapping

The scan is the whole bound volume, not just this article, and the two assigned index windows are **not** the same paper:

- **PDF index 258** (line 53238) = printed **p. 215**, the opening of the *first* installment: "On some New Genera and Species of Cystidea from the Trenton Limestonee / Read before the Canadian Institute, February 11th, by E. Billings..." — this is `1854a_billings` (genus *Glyptocystites*), ending at index ~261 (p. 218) with "(*To be continued.*)" and an errata note. It is **not spurious and not our paper** — it just happens to carry the identical article title, which is why it surfaced. Confirmed later in the scan at index 297 (p. 250) that `1854b_billings` reopens with "(*Concluded from page 218.*)" — i.e. installments 1854a+1854b together are Billings' own single "First Paper," serialized across the April and May issues.
- **PDF index 324–327** (lines 66616–67163) = printed **pp. 271–274**, squarely inside our assigned range. **PDF index 321–323** (lines 66191–66616), immediately upstream, carry printed **pp. 268–270** and were needed to reach the article's actual opening (title, "SECOND PAPER." heading, and the Comarocystites material) — the assignment's index-324 starting point lands mid-article, after the genus headers.
- Net offset in this stretch: printed page = PDF index − 53 (e.g. index 321 → p. 268, index 327 → p. 274), consistent across pp. 268–274. This offset is local to this stretch of the volume; it is not a fixed global constant (the p. 215 example above gives PDF index − printed page ≈ 43, showing the volume's front matter/plates shift the offset as the scan progresses).
- Confirmed printed page numbers seen directly in the OCR: 268 (inferred, boundary), 269, 270, 271, 272, 273, 274 — all present and in order, each preceded by the running head "NEW GENERA AND SPECIES OF CYSTIDEA." (or its recto/verso variants "SPECIES OF CYSTIDEA." / bracketed "[1854.]").
- The article that begins on p. 268 is explicitly headed "**SECOND PAPER.**" with "**Read before the Canadian Institute, April 8th, 1854.**" printed directly beneath the byline — this is `1854c_billings`.

## 4. Cases for the data model

**Comarocystites two-stage introduction (confirms the tree's note exactly).**
- Page 268, immediately under "SECOND PAPER. / Read before the Canadian Institute, April 8th, 1854.": "**GENUS COMAROCYSTITES.** When deeply imbedded in the rock, and without the column, arms, or ovarian aperture being visible, the fossil for which the above generic name is proposed, might, upon a superficial examination, be readily mistaken for a coral." — the name is given as a section header with narrative description following, no formal diagnosis yet.
- Page 269, after several paragraphs of comparative description: "I beg to suggest as a name for the genus, Comarocystites, from the Greek κόμαρον — a strawberry. Its characters may be concisely summed up as follows:— **GENUS COMAROCYSTITES.** (Greek κόμαρον, arbutum, and κύστις, vesica.) Body ovate, the smaller extremity being the base, pelvis small, of three plates, above which are from eight to eleven rows of plates, mostly hexagonal, ovarian aperture near the summit, closed by a valvular apparatus, mouth apical, arms free, composed of a single series of joints and bearing tentacula, column round." — this is the terse formal diagnosis.
- The tree's note ("First given on page 268, formal definition starts on page 269") is verified word-for-word correct.

**Agelacrinites placeholder treatment (C4).**
Text (p. 271–273): Billings reports finding, at one Bytown locality, "seven specimens of the fossil above described by Mr. Sowerby" (i.e. the *already-known*, previously undescribed-to-species American fossil that Forbes had referred to *Agelacrinites* — introduced on p. 268 of the paper's own recap, not new here), then gives an extensive comparative re-description over ~2 pages and concludes it "certainly seems to constitute a different genus" from true *Agelacrinites* — he does **not** name this large form, nor propose a new genus for it, leaving the question fully open. Separately, he reports smaller specimens found nearby that he *does* confidently attribute to genuine *Agelacrinites*: "Neither of the small specimens are in my possession, but judging from Vanuxem's figure and Professor Hall's description of Hemicystites, and also from recollection of the structure of the fossils, I have no doubt but that they are Agelucrinites [sic]." He never gives these a species name.
This is a textbook "unnamed" case per C4 — Billings believes a real taxon (the small specimens = *Agelacrinites*) is present but does not name it — so the `openTaxon: agelacrinites-sp_billings_1854` placeholder is the right pattern. However, the tree's single `agelacrinites` node collapses two textually distinct populations: (a) the small specimens Billings confidently calls *Agelacrinites* (matches the placeholder), and (b) the large, extensively-redescribed fossil he suspects is a *different, unnamed* genus, which the text discusses at far greater length but which is not represented anywhere in the tree because Billings never commits it to any genus, named or placeholder. Worth naming to the owner: this second population may warrant its own uncertain/unnamed-genus placeholder rather than being silently absent.
Also worth naming: Billings quotes Hall's own claim that *Hemicystites* Hall = *Agelacrinites* Vanuxem (Hall, in a note at p. 355 of Pal. N.Y. vol. 2: "This genus is apparently identical with Agelacrinites of Vanuxem, the description and figure of which I had overlooked..."). This is Hall's synonymy claim, relayed by Billings, not Billings' own act — a nuance for `notes` if ever captured.

**New-genus notation / punctuation (item 3).**
No "n. gen." / "n. sp." abbreviation and no parenthetical subgenus-as-citation device (the B6 "Heckerocrinus (Bockia)" pattern) appear anywhere in this installment. Billings introduces both new genera in full prose:
- Comarocystites: "I beg to suggest as a name for the genus, Comarocystites..." (etymological parenthetical follows the formal header only: "(Greek κόμαρον, arbutum, and κύστις, vesica.)")
- Amygdalocystites: "The most perfect, Fig. 4, much resembles an almond, and I therefore propose for it the generic name Amygdalocystites." — no etymological parenthetical at all here, unlike Comarocystites.
- Species headers ("Comarocystites punctuatus.", "Amygdalocystites florealis.", "Amygdalocystites radiatus.", "Amygdalocystites tenuistriatus.") are bare binomial headings with no "n. sp." marker.
By contrast, the *first* installment (1854a, p. 215) uses an explicit "(Now. gen.)" [OCR for "(Nov. gen.)"] marker before the *Glyptocystites* diagnosis — a notation convention Billings does **not** carry over into this Second Paper. Worth flagging as an inconsistency in Billings' own practice across the two papers, not a dataset error.

**Illustration/figure check (item 4).** See correctness table above — punctuatus fig. 2 omitted; florealis fig. 6 and the whole radiatus figure/page attribution are uncertain from OCR reading order and should be checked against the actual plate.

## 5. Source record check

- **"Limestonee" (double-e) typo — NOT a data-entry duplication error.** It is the genuine printed title, confirmed independently at two separate, non-adjacent locations in the scan: p. 215 ("On some New Genera and Species of Cystidea from the Trenton **Limestonee**") and again verbatim at p. 248 ("On some New Genera and Species of Cystidea from the Trenton **Limestonee** ... (Concluded from page 218.)"). Because it recurs identically across two independently-OCR'd pages of two different journal issues, it is far more likely a genuine compositor's typo reproduced (or re-set) for both halves of the same article than an OCR coincidence — though this cannot be settled with certainty without the physical page/plate image. **No owner action needed**; `1854a_billings`/`1854b_billings` correctly preserve the printed title as printed.
- **1854a/1854b shared `processDates.read: 1854-02-11`** — also **not an error**. Text confirms these are the two halves of one single paper read at one Institute meeting (p. 248: "Read before the Canadian Institute, February 11th," and "(Concluded from page 218.)"), simply serialized across the April and May issues of the Journal. The identical read date is correct.
- **1854c `pubDate.month: 6` vs `processDates.read: 1854-04-08`** — **confirmed correct, not a discrepancy.** The printed dateline under "SECOND PAPER." reads exactly: "**Read before the Canadian Institute, April 8th, 1854.**" This matches the source record's `read: 1854-04-08` precisely. The ~2-month gap to the June publication date is a normal read-to-print lag, not a data error.
- **Title/pages/volume/author check**: pages 268–274, vol. 2, author Billings all confirmed directly from the printed article. Byline reads "By [E.] Billings, Barrister at Law, Bytown, Canada West" (OCR renders the initial as "Le."), matching `authors: [billings]`.
- **A7/A8 citation-form note**: Billings' own numbering treats his work as two "papers" (a First Paper spanning 1854a+1854b, and this Second Paper), but this dataset keys three separate source records by journal issue (1854a April, 1854b May, 1854c June). This is a legitimate, deliberate modeling choice (source keys track publication instalments, not the author's own paper-numbering) but is worth naming explicitly since a reader cross-referencing Billings' internal "First Paper"/"Second Paper" language could otherwise expect only two source keys.
- **Minor title-construction note**: the printed article does not literally read "...Trenton Limestone, Second Paper" as one continuous title string — "SECOND PAPER." is a separate heading line below the title and byline. The source record's title field folds this into one string, which is a reasonable normalization but not a verbatim transcription; flagging only for awareness, not as an error.

## 6. Uncertainties

- Fig. 3's caption is OCR'd as "Fig. 8" ("A large specimen of Comarocystites punctuatus, retaining an arm and several tentacula..."); context (repeated cross-references elsewhere in the text to "the specimen (Fig. 3)") strongly implies this is OCR noise for "Fig. 3," but this is **cannot verify** without the plate image.
- The exact page (270 vs. 271) for *Amygdalocystites florealis* fig. 6 and for the whole of *A. radiatus* (heading, description, figs. 7–8) is **cannot verify** from OCR text alone — see correctness table. This journal interleaves figure-caption blocks with body text in a way that can scramble OCR reading order relative to the true page boundary; a check against the scanned page images (not just OCR text) is needed to resolve this with confidence.
- Whether the shared "Limestonee" typo reflects the original typesetting or a coincidental identical OCR error at two locations is **cannot verify** without viewing the original page images/plates.
- No output-side issues found in the `agelacrinites`/`agelacrinites-sp_billings_1854` C4 pattern match itself, but see §4 for the uncaptured "large form, possibly different genus" population — this is a coverage observation, not an error, and not something this audit can resolve without an editorial decision.
