## (a) Classifications printed

Haeckel prints classification as tabular "System der ..." sections, not
prose lists, at several nested levels. Transcribed here as nested lists with
his rank words and pages; table column detail (character states) is
mentioned only where it bears on identity, since the OCR on these
multi-column tables is unreliable (see (g)).

### Top level: System der Echinodermen (p. 164)

Rank words used: **Cladom** (highest), **Klasse**, **Subklasse oder Legion**.

- I. Cladom: Monorchonia (Thecozoa)
  - 1. Klasse: Amphoridea — 1A. Amphoralia (Archamphoria); 1B. Amphoronia (Cystamphoria)
  - 2. Klasse: Holothurea — 2A. Paractinota (Parholothuria); 2B. Actinopoda (Autholothuria)
  - 3. Klasse: Cystoidea — 3A. Microplacta (Eucystidea); 3B. Megaplacta (Parcystidea)
- II. Cladom: [name not legible in OCR]
  - 4. Klasse: Blastoidea — 4A. Eublastoida (Pentremitaria); 4B. Parblastoida (Astrocrinaria)
  - 5. Klasse: Urinoidea — 5A. Palaecrinida (Tessellata); 5B. Neocrinida (Articulata)
- III. Cladom: Pentorchonia (Pygocincta)
  - 6. Klasse: Echinidea — 6A. Palechinida (Palechinoidea); 6B. Autechinida (Euechinoidea)
  - 7. Klasse: Ophiurea — 7A. Palophiura (Palaeophiuroidea); 7B. Colophiura (Autophiuroidea)
  - 8. Klasse: Asteridea — 8A. Palasterida (Encrinasteria); 8B. Colasterida (Euasterida)

This is the whole-phylum system; only classes 1 (Amphoridea) and 3
(Cystoidea) are developed in detail in this paper.

### System der Amphoridea (p. 12), rank words **Ordo/Ordnung, Familia/Familie,
Subfamilia, Genus**

- I. Ordnung: Amphoralia (= Amphoridea bilateralia)
  - a) Amphoraeida
  - b) Pentactaeida
- (a second, unnamed-in-OCR ordnung for the sessile/monaxonic branch, per the
  Klasse-level table's "Amphoronia")

Four families, each with its own formal heading and synonymy (see (b)/(c)):

1. **Erste Familie der Amphorideen: Eocystida**, p. 30 (= Archaeocystida +
   Protamphorida, Haeckel 1895)
2. **Zweite Familie der Amphorideen: Anomocystida**, Woodward 1880 — p. 33

   Placocystida, Hkl.; II. Atelocystida, Hkl.; III. Pleurocystida, Hkl.
3. **Dritte Familie der Amphorideen: Aristocystida**, E. Haeckel 1895 (—
   non Neumayr —) — p. 45 — two Subfamiliae: Pirocystida, Orocystida
4. **Vierte Familie der Amphorideen: Palaeocystida**, E. Haeckel 1895 — p. 61

### System der Cystoidea (p. 77), same rank words

Two Ordines (Microplacta/Enucystidea; Megaplacta/Parcystidea, as at the
Klasse-level table), six families:

1. **Erste Familie der Cystoideen: Pomocystida**, E. Haeckel 1895 — p. 94
2. **Zweite Familie der Cystoideen: Fungocystida**, E. Haeckel 1895 — p. 101
3. **Dritte Familie der Cystoideen: Agelacystida** (= Agelacrinida),
   E. Haeckel 1895 — p. 107 — two Subfamiliae, proposed in prose ("unterscheide
   ich die Hemicystida und Asterocystida", p. 107): **Hemicystida** and
   **Asterocystida**; within Asterocystida a further Subfamilia
   **Edriocystida** is named in prose (p. 118) for the genus pair
   Edriocystis + Mesites.
4. **Vierte Familie der Cystoideen: Ascocystida**, E. Haeckel 1895 — p. 119
5. **Fünfte Familie der Cystoideen: Callocystida**, Bernard 1895 — p. 125
6. **Sechste Familie der Cystoideen: Glyptocystida**, E. Haeckel 1895 —
   p. 136 — two Subfamiliae: Hexalacystida (Caryocrinida), Sycocystida

Each family's genera are numbered continuously within its class (Amphoridea
genera and Cystoidea genera are two separate running series, each reaching
into the 30s–40s+; the printed register, pp. 178–179, lists roughly 150
genus/family names across both classes). Full genus-by-genus transcription
is not attempted here; see (b) for the genera explicitly marked new.

**Appendices** (per the Inhalts-Uebersicht, p. 180): I. Anhang: Systematische
Determination der Amphorideen und Cystoideen von Barrande; II. Anhang:
Camarocystida — Lobolithes (pp. 170–172, containing genus *Palmacystis*,
p. 151, and others).

## (b) Names proposed as new

Haeckel is explicit in the Vorwort (p. 6) that he does **not** propose new
species: "Weiter als bis zu den Gattungen hinabzugehen, schien mir nicht
rathsam" ("I did not think it advisable to go further down than to the
genera"). Confirmed by search: no "n. sp."/"nov. sp." anywhere in the text.
What follows is genus rank and above only.

**Note on what counts as new in 1896.** Most of the family-rank names
(Eocystida's components, Palaeocystida, Pomocystida, Fungocystida,
Agelacystida, Ascocystida, Glyptocystida) are explicitly cited by Haeckel
himself to his own preliminary paper, "Die cambrische Stammgruppe der
Echinodermen" (Jenaische Zeitschrift für Naturwissenschaft Bd. XXX, cited
throughout as reference "50"), **1895** — not new here. Example (p. 8):

> "Als die wirkliche Stammgruppe aller Echinodermen aber betrachte ich eine
> ältere und primitivere Klasse, welche ich als Amphoridea (oder
> „Urnensterne‘) bezeichnet habe (50, p.2)."

Genuinely new material in this 1896 paper:

Every "nov. gen." genus is given as a replacement/umbrella name for a
species (or species group) previously described under someone else's genus
— i.e., each is a new combination on an old species, never a new species.

## (c) Names taken from other authors

The family-rank headings are full synonymy blocks; representative examples:

- **Anomocystida**, credited to **Woodward, 1880** (p. 33), with a chain of
  independent later usages of essentially the same concept: "Anomalocystida,
  WOODWARD, 1880", "Pleurocystida, NEUMAYR, 1889", "Pleurocystida, BERNARD,
  1895", "Anomalocystida, ZITTEL, 1895", and finally "Anomocystida, E.
  HAECKEL, 1895" listed as one synonym among several — Haeckel explicitly
  gives priority to Woodward despite having used the identical spelling
  himself the year before.
- **Aristocystida**: Haeckel flags his own 1895 usage as distinct from an
  earlier, narrower use of the *same spelling* by Neumayr: "Aristocystida,
  E. HAECKEL (— non NEUMAYR, 8! —), 50, pag. 5." (p. 45). Prose (p. 46)
  explains that Neumayr's 1889 system split the same material into three
  families (Aristocystida, Sphaeronitida, Echinosphaerida sensu Neumayr),
  which Haeckel's broader "Aristocystida" concept unifies.
- **Callocystida**, credited to **Bernard, 1895** (p. 125): "Callocystida,
  BERNARD, 1895; 30, pag. 206", with Zittel 1895 as a parallel usage.
- Genus-level adoptions with full original citation, e.g. "12. Genus:
  Hemicystis, Hall, 1852" (p. 111); "13. Genus: Agelacrinus, Vanuxem, 1842"
  (p. 112); "14. Genus: Lepidodiscus, Hall, 1872" (p. 113); "16. Genus:
  Cyathocystis, F. Schmidt, 1879" (p. 114); "17. Genus: Gomphocystis, Hall,
  1868" (p. 114); "19. Genus: Asteroblastus, Eichwald, 1861" (p. 117); "21.
  Genus: Mesites, Hoffmann, 1866" (p. 118).
- Otto Jaekel's 1895 proposal to remove the Hemicystida-type forms into a
  separate class "Thecoidea" is cited and explicitly rejected (see (d)).

## (d) Treatment of the edrioasteroid-relevant names

**Cyclocystoides, Thyroidea, Pyrgocystis, Stromatocystites: not mentioned
anywhere in this work** (checked by full-text search, 0 hits each).

**Agelacrinites** (3 hits) and **Hemicystites** (1 hit) appear only inside
synonymy lines, not as accepted genus headings:
- "Agelacrinites, BARRANDE, 12, pag. 83, Pl. 37" is listed as a synonym
  under **genus Hemicystis** (Hall, 1852) — i.e. Haeckel places Barrande's
  Bohemian *Agelacrinites* material in *Hemicystis*, not in *Agelacrinus*
  (p. 111).
- "Agelacrinites, VANUXEM, Geol. Report New-York, pag. 168" is listed as
  Vanuxem's own original spelling, synonymized under **genus Agelacrinus**
  Vanuxem 1842 (p. 112) — consistent with the existing `taxa.yaml` note on
  `agelacrinus` (Bell 1976: Agelacrinites commonly misspelled Agelacrinus),
  since here both spellings trace to Vanuxem himself.
- "Hemicystites, Hall, [Palaeontology of New York] Vol. II, pag. 245" is a
  synonym under genus Hemicystis (p. 111).

**Agelacrinus** Vanuxem 1842 (genus, p. 112) is restricted by Haeckel to
species with all five ambulacra curved the same way; type species
*Agelacrinus vorticellatus* Hall 1872. Forbes' *Agelacrinus Buchianus* is
explicitly moved out: "gehört wahrscheinlich nicht hierher, sondern zu
Edriocystis" (p. 113).

**Edriocystis**, new genus (p. 117; see (b)), is presented as a replacement
for **Edrioaster Billings, 1858** and its synonym **Cyclaster Billings,
1856** (Report Geol. Survey Canada, p. 292), carrying the same type species
under a new combination: "Species typica: Edriocystis Bigsbyi, E. Haeckel. —
Edrioaster Bigsbyi, BILLINGS, 15, pag. 82." (p. 118).

**All of this material is kept inside Cystoidea**, as one family
(Agelacystida, = Agelacrinida) with two subfamilies (Hemicystida,
Asterocystida — the latter containing the further subfamily Edriocystida
for *Edriocystis* + *Mesites*). Haeckel explicitly rejects raising any part
of it to class rank, citing and disagreeing with Jaekel:

> "Otto Jaekel (49, pag. 110) hält die Hemicystiden ... für 'die
> primitivsten Formen der Pelmatozoen' und der Echinodermen überhaupt; er
> bildet aus ihnen die besondere Klasse der Thecoidea. Nach meiner Auffassung
> dagegen gehören dieselben zu den höchst entwickelten Formen der
> Cystoideen..." (p. 108)

So: no separate "edrioasteroid" taxon of any rank is recognized in this
paper; the group is nested family/subfamily/genus inside Cystoidea, and a
contemporary's proposal to split it off (Jaekel's Thecoidea) is named and
rejected on the page.

**Cyathocystis** (4 hits, genus, Schmidt 1879 — adopted, p. 114) is grouped
in the same subfamily complex, noted as lacking double pores like
*Edriocystis* (p. 109) and having partly fused plates "ähnlich wie bei
einigen Formen von Gomphocystis" (p. 114).

## (e) Diagnoses

Haeckel never uses the word "Diagnose." Every family gets a formal, labeled
**"Familien-Charakter:"** paragraph (German prose, unlabeled sub-parts, e.g.
p. 33: "Familien-Charakter: Amphorideen mit bilateraler, stark dorso-ventral
deprimirter Theca..."). Every genus follows a fixed, unlabeled template:
number + name + author + year → synonymy lines → plate/figure citation →
one prose character paragraph → "Species typica:" with the type species'
own synonymy → "Fundort:" (locality). No Latin diagnoses are printed; all
characterization is German prose plus the tabular "System der ..." columns
(which give character states in table-cell form rather than prose — see (g)
for OCR reliability on those tables).

## (g) Uncertainties / OCR

The scan mixes two very different reliability levels:
- **All-caps title-page and running-head text OCRs essentially perfectly**
  (the title-page transcription above has zero errors).
- **Ordinary running prose OCRs poorly and inconsistently.** The author's
  own name, always printed in letter-spaced small caps ("Ernst Haeckel"),
  comes back as at least six different garbled forms across the text:
  "Harorer", "Harcker", "Hascker", "Harckrer", "Harcken", "IHazeren" — vs.
  clean "E. HAECKEL" in the full-caps synonymy citations. Other frequent,
  systematic substitutions: c↔e ("Cystoidea"→"Uystoideen", "Eocystis"→
  "Eoeystis"), missing/altered letters at word ends ("Hall"→"Harn"/"Haın",
  "Barrande"→"Barranoe"/"Barkasoe"), and occasional digit misreads in
  running-head page numbers (see the page-mapping note above). This pattern
  — clean caps/Antiqua, unreliable lower-case running text — is consistent
  with a **Fraktur (German blackletter) body face** being read by an
  OCR model that handles Antiqua/Roman capitals well but not blackletter
  minuscules; it is not consistent with a fully Antiqua-set page. Genus and
  species Latin names in the synonymy blocks (set in a distinct, probably
  Antiqua/italic type) are markedly more reliable than the surrounding
  German prose, but still not error-free (e.g. "Aristoeystida" for
  "Aristocystida", "mov. gen." for "nov. gen.").
- Practical effect: every proper name and every year quoted above was
  checked in context against a synonymy line or a repeated occurrence
  elsewhere in the same passage; where a name occurs only once and the OCR
  reading looks unstable, it is flagged inline above rather than silently
  corrected (e.g. "Eocystida, E. Harorer." with no year — cannot verify
  whether a year was printed and dropped by OCR, or genuinely omitted).
- The "System der ..." tables are multi-column layouts (characters |
  genera) that the OCR linearizes into a single column, interleaving rows
  from different columns. The names and page numbers pulled from them above
  are reliable (they recur as plain genus headings elsewhere), but the
  table's character-state text is not reproduced here because the column
  order cannot be trusted from this OCR.
- The Inhalts-Uebersicht (p. [180]) prints section titles and their page
  numbers as two separately OCR'd blocks that had to be re-paired by
  position; the pairing used above matches the section pages found
  independently in the body text, so it is treated as reliable.
- One Cladom name (the second of three, covering Blastoidea/Urinoidea) did
  not OCR into anything legible — left blank in (a), cannot verify.

## Cases for the data model

- **B18 (same name, different rank/spelling).** Haeckel's Klasse
  "Amphoridea" is exactly the phenomenon B18 describes for *Rhombifera*: the
  `taxa.yaml` record (`amphorida`, rank Order) reflects Bather 1900's later
  usage (`data/trees/1900_bather.yaml` line 25, an Order-level child of
  Cystidea, noted "Haekel (1896, pars)"), not Haeckel's own rank (Klasse) or
  spelling (Amphoridea). If this source is entered, its own tree node would
  need `rank: Klasse`, name resolving to a record spelled "Amphoridea" —
  either a new record or an `altSpellingOf`/rank link to the existing
  `amphorida`, per B18's "one record per name-and-authority-and-rank" rule.
- **A6/A8-style self-citation.** Haeckel repeatedly cites his own earlier
  paper by number ("50, pag. X") for names he is reusing rather than
  proposing (Amphoridea p.8, Eocystida's components p.30, Palaeocystida
  p.61, Pomocystida p.94, etc.). No `1895_haeckel` (or equivalent) source
  record exists yet in `sources.yaml`/`data/publications.yaml` to resolve
  these citations against; entering this tree would need either that source
  record or an `editorial.source` note explaining the citation cannot yet
  resolve.
- **Independent same-spelling family names (not quite B10, not quite
  B18).** "Aristocystida" is used by both Neumayr (1889, narrower) and
  Haeckel (1895/96, broader) for overlapping but non-identical material;
  Haeckel's synonymy line itself flags this with "(— non NEUMAYR, 8! —)".
  This is a same-rank, same-spelling, different-circumscription case
  volunteered by the source itself — worth a named home in the roadmap if a
  second example turns up (an author explicitly disclaiming coincidence
  with an unrelated homonymous coinage at the same rank).
- **Mixed novelty-marking conventions.** In one work, "new" is signalled
  three different ways depending on rank/context: an explicit "(nov. gen.)"
  tag at genus rank (the majority of new genera); a bare author abbreviation
  "Hkl." with no year at all inside a table heading (the three new
  Anomocystida subfamilies, p. 37); and a first-person proposing verb in
  prose with no tag ("unterscheide ich", p. 107, for two Cystoidea
  subfamilies; "gründe ich", p. 134, for genus Staurocystis). A tree entered
  from this source would need `new: true` set from prose/verb evidence as
  often as from an explicit "nov."
- **Nomen-novum-like replacement without the label.** *Edriocystis* (p. 117)
  replaces Billings' *Edrioaster* 1858 (itself a replacement for his own
  *Cyclaster* 1856) with the identical type species, but Haeckel neither
  writes "nom. nov." nor gives a year on the genus line — only the fact that
  no prior Haeckel citation exists for it distinguishes it from an ordinary
  retained-genus heading. This is the B6/B27 territory ("new name" acts
  printed without the technical phrase) but at genus rank rather than
  family.
- **Species-level moves without new species.** Forbes' *Agelacrinus
  Buchianus* is reassigned to *Edriocystis* in a single sentence (p. 113,
  "gehört wahrscheinlich nicht hierher, sondern zu Edriocystis") — a
  `moved`-type placement change with no new combination formally erected
  (no "comb. nov." language, just a stated opinion, itself hedged
  "wahrscheinlich").
- **G7-adjacent point.** The *Hemicystis*/*Agelacrinites* synonymy tangle
  that G7 already documents for Hall 1852 (addenda-only synonymy, p. 300)
  recurs here as a second, independent synonymy call: Haeckel puts
  Barrande's *Agelacrinites* under *Hemicystis* (p. 111) while keeping
  Vanuxem's *Agelacrinites* under *Agelacrinus* (p. 112) — two same-named
  usages resolved to two different accepted genera by the same author on
  facing pages. Both are printed synonymy claims, not the addenda case
  itself, but the same genus complex is now visited by a second, unrelated
  reviewer's synonymy decisions.
- **Festschrift chapter as a source-record shape.** No source in this
  corpus currently records a Festschrift; `1857_billings`'s `chapter:` +
  `pages:` pattern (report within a larger annual volume) is the closest
  existing shape but doesn't capture "own pagination within a multi-author
  dedicatory volume." Worth deciding before entry, since the BHL page link
  in the current record points at a single page and gives no page-range
  context.
