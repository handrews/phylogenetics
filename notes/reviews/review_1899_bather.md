## The classification as printed

The paper opens (pp. 916–918) with unstructured phylogenetic argument, not
itself a classification: a diagram of "mutual relations of the Classes" on
p. 917 (Pelmatozoa {Cystidea, Edrioasteroidea, Blastoidea, Crinoidea} vs.
"(Eleutherozoa)" {Holothurioidea, Echinoidea, Stelleroidea}), and prose
establishing Diploporita/Rhombifera/Aporita among Cystidea and Protoblastoidea
vs. Eublastoidea among Blastoidea, plus a Crinoidea phylogeny diagram (p. 918,
"Supposed Relations of the Orders and Sub-Orders of Crinoidea"). None of this
is the formal classification; it is announced separately: "On the foregoing
principles and beliefs, along with many others related in the book itself,
is based the following classification of Pelmatozoa:—" (p. 919). "The book
itself" is the forthcoming Lankester *Treatise* — see 1900_bather review.

## Source record check

Title, journal reference, volume title, and pages all match the printed
volume: title page reads "REPORT OF THE SIXTY-[E]IGHTH MEETING OF THE
BRITISH ASSOCIATION [FOR THE] ADVANCEMENT OF SCIENCE[, HELD AT] BRISTOL[,] IN
SEPTEMBER 1898" (OCR drops small connecting words — **cannot verify "held
at" was actually printed there**, but "68th", "Bristol", "September 1898"
are unambiguous), imprint "LONDON: JOHN MURRAY... 1899." `pages: [916, 923]`
is confirmed twice over: by the paper's own boundaries in the volume, and by
Bather's own bibliography entry in the 1900 book (item 12, p. 216): "1899.
(A phylogenetic classification of the Pelmatozoa.) Rep. Brit. Assoc. for
1898, pp. 916-923." `authors: [bather]` matches (sole author).

`processDates.conferenceStart` and `conferenceEnd` are both given as
`1898-09-07`. The volume's Section D running order shows sittings on
Thursday 8, Friday 9, Saturday 10, Monday 12, Tuesday 13, and Wednesday 14
September 1898 (the same six-day pattern repeats across every Section in the
volume), and Bather's paper was read specifically in the **Tuesday, 13
September 1898** sitting, as item 5. Whether 7 September was the meeting's
ceremonial opening day is plausible but **not confirmed from the pages
read** (the front-matter "Places and Times of Meeting" table, which would
settle it, was not among the pages examined). Either way, a single identical
date for both `conferenceStart` and `conferenceEnd` does not represent a
meeting whose sectional business ran across at least six days, and does not
capture this specific paper's actual reading date of 13 September.

verify**, not fetched.

## The year problem

`sources.yaml` dates the printed report `1899` and the conference `1898-09-07`
— both correct as far as this review can confirm (the volume's imprint is
1899; the conference was held in September 1898). The difficulty is that
later authors, and this dataset's own `taxa.yaml`, cite names from this
single paper under **both** years, and the paper itself gives no help: no
name in the printed classification carries a year at all. Three pieces of
direct evidence bear on which year Bather himself thought correct for citing
his own work:

1. Bather's own bibliography in the 1900 book (item 12, p. 216) lists this
   exact paper as **"1899."**, with "*Rep. Brit. Assoc. for 1898*" describing
   the venue, not the citation year.
2. In the same 1900 book, Bather cites two names from this paper under
   **"1899"**: the grade "Protoblastoidea, Bather (1899)" (p. 79) and his
   own earlier placement "the reference of *Steganoblastus* to the
   Protoblastoidea (Bather, 1899)" (p. 209).
3. In the same 1900 book, Bather cites a third name from the very same
   paper under **"1898"**: "*Dinocystis*, Bather (1898)" (p. 209). The class
   heading also credits himself as one of three co-authors of
   Edrioasteroidea's separation as "BATHER, 1899" (p. 205) — i.e. even the
   two self-citations that use "1899" and "1898" both occur within pages of
   each other.

So Bather himself was not consistent about which year to attach to acts from
this one paper, within the space of a single later book. `taxa.yaml`
currently follows the "1898" convention for `edrioasteridae`, `edrioasterina`
(not found in either paper — see above) and `dinocystis` (matching Bather's
own "1898" self-citation), and the "1899" convention for `cyathocystidae`,
`protoblastoidea`, `macrocystellidae`, etc. (matching Bather's "1899"
self-citation and his own bibliography entry). Both conventions are
independently attested in Bather's own later usage; **the printed 1899
paper itself supports neither year over the other for any specific name**,
since it prints no years at all. Per A12/A8 in the roadmap, each printed
citation should stand as printed and the comparison stay derived — this
paper simply gives no printed citation to record for its own names.

## Cases for the data model

**No printed "new" marker anywhere.** Every name in the classification is
presented as an epitome of "a forthcoming text-book... edited by Professor
Ray Lankester" (p. 916), with no "n. fam.", "gen. nov." or similar. Coverage
of "new taxa" for this source cannot be done by scanning for a marker; it
requires cross-checking every name against prior literature, exactly as the
`taxa.yaml` compilation above does, imperfectly. This is a case for B24-style
documentation of hedged/unmarked protologues, but stronger: not hedged, just
silent.

**A source that "epitomises" a forthcoming book.** The paper explicitly
says it is a preview: "The classification is an epitome of that adopted in a
forthcoming text-book of zoology edited by Professor Ray Lankester" (p. 916).
This is the direct link to `1900_bather`; see that review's comparison
section. Not covered by any existing roadmap item — closest is G7 (a tree
assembled from several printed structures), but here two *separate sources*
share one classification, one anticipating the other. Worth a `notes` cross-
reference on both sources when `1899_bather` is entered.

**A likely wrong genus authority reachable only by comparing the two
papers.** `taxa.yaml`'s `haplocystis` record credits the genus to Bather
1899, but Bather's own 1900 book explicitly credits it to "C. F. Roemer
(1855)" (p. 208). The 1899 paper only *lists* Haplocystis, undifferentiated
from other genera in the family's genus enumeration, with no attribution and
no "new" marker; nothing on that page suggests the genus is Bather's own.
This looks like a plain error, discoverable only because both papers are in
hand — exactly what the review round is for. See also `glyptocystinae`,
already flagged uncertain by the editor: this 1899 paper (p. 920) is a
reasonable candidate source for "Bather, 1899" rather than the currently
recorded "Calvin, 1899", since it appears in Bather's own sentence
alongside Callocystinae (independently confirmed as Bernard 1895) and
Echinoencrininae — but this is a plausibility argument, not proof; the
actual Calvin reference was not checked.

**A family-rank name apparently pre-dated by this paper.** `taxa.yaml`
credits `tiaracrinidae` to `1900_bather`, but the family (as "Tiaracrinide")
is already printed here, in 1899, with the same lack of attribution as every
other family in the paper. Since neither paper marks it "new," and the genus
*Tiaracrinus* is much older (Schultze 1867, per the 1900 book), the family
name's own date of origin is genuinely unclear from these two sources; what
is clear is that crediting it to 1900 cannot be right if 1899 already prints
it unremarked, unless some other, unexamined publication used it earlier
still. Worth flagging for correction to `1899_bather` pending a check of
whether any pre-1899 source used "Tiaracrinidae".

**Coordinate names within one taxon.** *Steganoblastus* appears in this
paper (p. 921) only as a genus, bracketed with a "[?]" inside family
Asteroblastidae under Protoblastoidea. The 1900 book moves it to its own
family, Steganoblastidae, under Edrioasteroidea, citing this very paper for
the *earlier* placement it is now overturning ("(Bather, 1899)", p. 209).
This is a clean example of a taxon changing class between two sources by the
same author one year apart — see the 1900 review's comparison section for
the full citation.

**An apparently new intermediate rank with no formal name-act language.**
"Series" (Codonoblastida, Troostoblastida, Granatoblastida) sits between
Grade and Family in the Blastoidea classification (pp. 921–922), introduced
by unmarked prose reasoning on p. 918. None of the three is in `taxa.yaml`.
If this source is entered, these three names will need `rank: Series`
records with no citable prior author — a clean case for A10's "silence is
not a choice": nothing here forces an attribution, but the rank word itself
is worth preserving as printed.
