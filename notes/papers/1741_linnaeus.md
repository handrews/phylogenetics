We observed *Primula* 2 growing only where the ground was cold, and the grass there remained short.

It was claimed that flax would not grow here because the land was too low-lying and damp—an excuse offered by the local farmer whenever his womenfolk were criticized for wearing *överdelar* (bodices or upper garments) as part of their daily attire; for the few women who actually owned linen garments did not wear such bodices.

We might call "Neptune’s Fields" a certain tract of land located an eighth of a mile from Torp, right by the seashore; it was about one musket-shot in width and several musket-shots in length. This area resembled the cultivated fields of Skåne or Uppland, where the soil is ridged to allow for drainage. Indeed, the resemblance was so striking that one could have sworn it was the work of a plow—until, upon closer inspection by hand, one discovered it consisted entirely of gravel—crushed fragments of limestone cast far inland by storms and sea waves—yet arranged, as the water receded, in a manner indistinguishable from a plowed field.

The lime kilns, visible here and there along the shore, were generally 5 *alnar* (ells) high and 4 *alnar* wide. The smaller kilns were inclined toward the ground, making it easier to feed in the wood and fuel. During the firing process, we noticed that the stones at the very top of the kiln appeared to be covered in a layer of white frost. The local people told us it was *falaska* [lime residue/ash], but
when we removed the hot stones, we discovered it was a
white salt—*alkali calcareum & fixum*—which had
sublimated here, much like *flores sulphuris* [flowers of sulfur] near lime-roasting kilns.

The quarries for this lime were nothing more
than the very edge of the shore—often a quarter-ell
below the water itself—where the limestone was extracted
with a crowbar and hammer, with little effort. When the
slabs were split apart, *darter* (or *Öland spikes*) were
embedded as densely as chaff in coarse bread; truly, God
alone knows where so many rare shells could have come from.

A type of fossil—quite rare elsewhere, though
common here—resembled a *valvulam echini* [sea urchin valve]; often as large
as the palm of a hand, it otherwise looked like a half-moon
with two parallel grooves and several transverse striations.

The spar crystals found here within the *darter* fossils
had tips shaped like those of saltpeter crystals,
yet they lacked a central column.

The women, who hauled the stone using oxen,
knitted stockings incessantly—even while
holding the reins as they drove.

The *Vigeltornet* [a large, ancient stone cairn or beacon], situated
near the coastal ridge some distance
from the lime kilns, was the largest we had ever seen;
it stood over three fathoms high,
with a circumference of five quarter-ells.
