The characteristics I have above mentioned, which are common to all known species of the Echinodermata, apply equally to the animals of the second order, of which Calcareous Balls and Crystallaceous Shells are petrified: Except that none of them are endowed with the few Spinae, with which Echinodermata are accustomed to move; and that the species described above is also without pores, and consequently without such tentacles, with which Echinodermata probe the bottom and attach themselves to other bodies.
It is clear that if these two characteristic marks are not necessary in each and every species of the genus Echini, the originals of the Kalk-balls cannot be brought under that family: and if they nevertheless come close to this classification, they could still less be referred to something else, without constituting a separate one. But since the genera are arbitrary, it is not necessary that the two above-mentioned characteristic marks (δ, ε) should form any part of the character of the genus Echini: and if they are omitted, the originals of the Kalk-balls should certainly have a place there.

The descriptions themselves are few in mind, that they do equally good service under whatever genus these species are also brought under.

Among the species of the Echinodermata, few can, as described in § 2., be easily distinguished from all previously known species of the genus, Cute pororum paribus sparsis undique pertusa:

But the other, whose description constitutes the 3rd § cute poris nullis vifibilibus pertufa: by which characteristics both species can be perfectly distinguished from each other. Although these characteristics are not the only ones by which the prototypes of the calcareous balls can be distinguished from all known Echinodermata; few need and should, however, not be used, after they now provide for this purpose, sufficient, and remain so, until new species can be discovered, in which these characteristics are also found.

From the descriptions of these species it can be gathered that they are, in several respects, remarkably different, few being found among them, from all known species of Echinus; to which they nevertheless come nearer than to any other species of animal. Thus it is difficult yet to discover distinct species of this species, by which both these species can be related, few being found: among them, from the species of the Echinus-flag, which we now know: for nature does not make mistakes.

For the sake of reason, and because of the similarity with the now accepted way of characterizing natural animals, I may be permitted to distinguish the former with the name of Echinus Pomum (k); and with Echinus Aurantium (1) the more familiar species.

But doubtless they live under very deep sea-water; For neither they themselves, nor any of the many species; which are petrified in their beds, are ever brought up to the sea-shores.
