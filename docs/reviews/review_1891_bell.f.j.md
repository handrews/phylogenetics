## (c) Names proposed as new

**Eleutherozoa** — the naming sentence (p. 210): "…the other leads to the
Echinoidea, Asteroidea, and Ophiuroidea; the former may be called the
Statozoa, the latter the Eleutherozoa." Formal diagnosis (p. 214): "The
Eleutherozoa are actinogonidiate caliculate Echinodermata in which the oral
surface of the body looks downwards, the power of locomotion is retained,
and the podia are often locomotor in function; the anus, if present, varies
in position." `eleutherozoa` already exists in `data/taxa.yaml`
(`auth: [Bell]`, `year: 1891`, `rank: Subphylum`) — this is that record's
source.

**Statozoa** — same naming sentence as above (p. 210), shared with
Eleutherozoa. Formal diagnosis (p. 214): "The Statozoa are actinogonidiate
caliculate Echinodermata in which the oral surface of the body looks
upwards, the body is temporarily or permanently fixed, the podia have a
respiratory function only, and the anus opens on the oral surface. They may
(pelmatozoic) or may not (apelmatozoic) have or have had a stalk." No taxon
record exists for this name.

**Incaliculata / Caliculata** — introduced as the two Branch headings of
"the proposed arrangement" (p. 212), no separate proposing sentence beyond
that framing (p. 211–212: "Put in the ordinary linear way the proposed
arrangement will read thus:—"). Diagnosed p. 213: "The Incaliculata are
Echinodermata in which no system of plates set alternately along and
between the rays is developed in the aboral region." / "The Caliculata are
Echinodermata in which the skeleton is always, in part at least, formed of
plates, some of which are set in rows, alternately radial and interradial,
round a single central plate." The underlying adjectives ("non-caliculate",
p. 207; "caliculate", p. 209) predate the substantive Branch names within
this same paper. No taxon records exist.

**Anactinogonidiata / Actinogonidiata** — likewise introduced as Stage
headings in the table (p. 212–213), diagnosed p. 213–214: "The
Anactinogonidiata are Echinodermata in which the vascular and nervous, but
not the digestive or reproductive, systems exhibit quinqueradiate symmetry."
/ "The Actinogonidiata are caliculate Echinodermata in which the generative
organs are radial in position or have undergone fusion and become
interradial." The underlying adjectives ("actinogonidiate"/
"anactinogonidiate") are coined earlier in the same paper, p. 207. No taxon
records exist.

**Zygopoda / Azygopoda** — the adjectives are proposed first (p. 211): "I am
unable to find a very satisfactory term for this arrangement, but I propose,
provisionally at any rate, to speak of it as zygopodous in the Urchin and
azygopodous in the Starfish." The substantive Division names appear in the
table (p. 213) and are diagnosed p. 214: "The Zygopoda are Eleutherozoa in
which the podia extend more or less uninterruptedly from the calycinal to
the oral region." / "The Azygopoda are Eleutherozoa in which the podia are
all or nearly all on the oral surface of the body only, and are separated by
terminal plates from any contact with the calycinal area." No taxon records
exist. Azygopoda's table line further reads "(s. Stelleridea, s. em.)" — a
cross-reference to "the Stelleridea…of earlier writers" (p. 212, see (e)),
"sensu emendato".

**Not new here.** Pelmatozoa: "By Pelmatozoa we have recently meant
Echinoderms fixed by their aboral pole" (p. 209–210) — presented as existing
usage, and explicitly credited to Leuckart's concept (see (e)); the existing
`pelmatozoa` record (authority 1848 Leuckart) is unaffected. Echinozoa:
"the present current classification into Pelmatozoa and Echinozoa" (p. 206)
and "the Echinozoa in the sense of some authors" (p. 210) — both describe
existing, not proposed, usage; Bell in fact argues against retaining
Echinozoa as a natural group (p. 210): "we cannot find sufficient
justification for their union under the common name Echinozoa." Stelleridea:
explicitly "of earlier writers" (p. 212).

## (d) Rank vocabulary versus the schema enum

Bell's table uses five distinct rank-like words (a sixth, "Sub-branch",
"Sub-stage" counted separately from "Branch"/"Stage" since they are visibly
distinct levels, not abbreviations). Checked against `$defs/rank` in
`schemas/phylogeny.yaml`:

| printed word | example (page) | level in Bell's hierarchy | in enum? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Branch | "Branch A. INCALICULATA." (p. 212) | top level, two branches | **yes** (`Branch`) |
| Stage | "Stage a. ANACTINOGONIDIATA." (p. 212) | second level, under Branch | **no** |
| Sub-branch | "1st Sub-branch. Svarozoa [Statozoa]." (p. 212) | third level, under Stage β | **no** |
| Sub-stage | "Sub-stage i. Apelmatozoic." (p. 213) | fourth level, under 1st Sub-branch only | **no** |
| Division | "Division i. Zygopoda." (p. 213) | third level, under 2nd Sub-branch only (parallel in tree depth to Sub-stage, but a different word) | **yes** (`Division`) |
| Class | "Class 1. Holothurioidea." (p. 212) | terminal level throughout | **yes** (`Class`) |

So three of the six printed words — **Stage**, **Sub-branch**, **Sub-stage**
— have no value in the enum (which otherwise already carries `Branch`,
`Division`, `Grade`, `Group`, `Unranked`, per the assignment). Note that
Bell uses "Division" and "Sub-stage" for what is structurally the same
tree depth on the two different sides of his Stage β split (pelmatozoic
Statozoa side uses Sub-stage; Eleutherozoa side uses Division) — the printed
words are not rank-equivalent labels reused consistently across the whole
tree, only within each branch.

The word "group(s)" also appears repeatedly in Bell's discursive prose
(e.g. "the groups of the Echinozoa among themselves", p. 206–207; "the
essential characters of the groups of Echinoderms", p. 212) but never as a
capitalised, numbered heading the way Branch/Stage/Class are used — it reads
as ordinary English, not a rank word of the classification, so it is not
counted as a printed rank term for this table.
