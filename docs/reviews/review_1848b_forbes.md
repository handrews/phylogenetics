## 4. Cases for the data model

**Subgenus/parenthetical notation (item 2).** No ICZN-style "Genus (Subgenus) species Author" parenthetical usage occurs anywhere in the genus/species headers examined (`Pseudocrinites`, `Apiocystites`, `Prunocystites`, `Echino-encrinus`/`Echino-encrinites`, `Hemicosmites`, `Caryocystites`, `Sphæronites`/`Echino-sphærites`, `Agelacrinites`). The one parenthetical pattern found is at the `Sphæronites` genus header (p.514/515):

> "SPHÆRONITES (Hisinger), or ECHINO-SPHÆRITES (Wahlemberg)."

This is a single header presenting two alternative generic names joined by "or," each followed by its respective authority **in parentheses** — an authority citation, not subgenus notation. It confirms `sphæronites: {or: [echino-sphærites]}` (tree lines 84–86) exactly: one printed heading, not two separate genus headers, correctly modeled as an `or` alternative rather than as synonymy.

**Sowerby placeholder citation (item 1).** Forbes never gives the Canadian Bigsby/Sowerby fossil a taxonomic name at genus or species level. He narrates it in prose ("a remarkable American fossil, figured and described by Mr. G. B. Sowerby, in the second volume of the Zoological Journal"), quotes Sowerby's original description verbatim in full (including Sowerby's own hedge, "we think... it would be properly placed among the genera of the Asteriada"), and cites it in a footnote: "*Zoological Journal, vol. ii., p. 318, pl. 2, fig. 5." (p.520). The tree correctly represents this as an unnamed `openTaxon` at both genus level (synonym of `agelacrinites`) and species level (synonym of the new placeholder `agelacrinites-sp_forbes_1848`), and the pattern is applied identically at both levels (bare `openTaxon:` key, no `pages`/`citedAs`/`notes`). Two findings: (a) the citation detail Forbes himself prints (p.318, pl.2, fig.5) is not captured on either node, despite being unusually well-documented for a synonym entry; (b) Forbes' footnote plate number ("pl. 2") does not match the plate recorded in `data/trees/1825b_sowerby.g.b.yaml` for the same figure (`illustrations: [{plate: XI, figures: 5}]`) — see item 7 below.

**Truncated diagnosis (item 5).** Confirmed genuine truncation of captured text, not an OCR-illegibility issue — see correctness table above.

**Doubled `oblongus_pander_1830` / `granatum_wahlenberg_1821` entries (item 6).** Both confirmed correct instances of the same recombination pattern: the accepted (if doubtful, for `oblongus_pander_1830`) placement under the current genus, plus a synonym entry recording the cited older combination under the genus the name was originally published in. See correctness table.

**Genus-level hedges not captured as notes (new finding, B16-adjacent).** Two blanket "provisional" hedges attach to entire genus-level species lists — Hemicosmites (p.510, "I provisionally refer to this genus the following fossils") and Caryocystites (p.512, "With the exception of the first species in the list, the descriptions given must be regarded merely as provisional") — and neither is captured in `notes` on the respective parent node, though B16 explicitly calls for the list-level hedge to be recorded there, quoted. Individual `provisional` flags on "?"-marked species are correctly unaffected by this gap.

**Prose-only "provisionally" hedges without a printed "?" (new finding, B16-adjacent).** Two new species — `Prunocystites Fletcheri` (p.504, "I have named it provisionally...") and `Sphæronites arachnoideus` (p.518, "I name it provisionally as above...") — are hedged by Forbes in prose only, with no "?" in the header. The tree's `provisional` flag is applied only where a printed "?" appears (`squamosus`, `oblongus_pander`, `punctatus`, `munitus`), so these two carry no `provisional` flag. Worth flagging for consistency, since the model's own convention (§B16, "provisional only on items actually marked '?'") appears to treat prose-only hedges as out of scope for the flag — but that leaves a real textual hedge unrecorded anywhere on these two nodes (no `notes` either).

**Echino-encrinus/Echino-encrinites usage (item 7-adjacent).** The tree's `notes` on `echino-encrinus` (lines 37–40) state the "definitions" use "Echino-encrinus" and the "commentary" uses "Echino-encrinites." The printed text is the reverse: the formal Latin diagnosis/essential-characters section is explicitly headed **"ECHINO-ENCRINITES."** (p.509, "The essential characters of the genus Echino-encrinites, and its British species, may be summed up as follows:—"), while the general section header and most running prose use **"ECHINO-ENCRINUS."** (p.504 section header; "Species of the genus Echino-encrinus have hitherto been found only in Russia and in the United States of America," p.506). The note's assignment of which form goes with "definitions" vs. "commentary" appears backwards from what is printed.

## 5. Source record check

`data/sources.yaml` key `1848b_forbes`: `title: On the Cystideæ of the Silurian Rocks of the British Islands.pdf` — the trailing **".pdf" is a likely artifact/typo**, not part of the actual title (compare the printed title, "On the CYSTIDEÆ of the Silurian Rocks of the British Islands," p.483, no ".pdf"). Otherwise the record matches the printed source: `journal: mem-geol-gb` resolves correctly to "Memoirs of the Geological Survey of Great Britain, and of the Museum of Practical Geology in London"; `volume: 2, number: 2` matches printed "II." pagination marks; `pages: [483, 538]` matches the printed page range exactly (opening "[483]" and closing plate-explanation page "538"); `plates: [11, 23]` is consistent with in-text plate citations (Plate 20, Plate 21 for Caryocystites; Plate XXIII wholly devoted to `Agelacrinites Buchianus`, p.538); `authors: [forbes]` matches the sole named author (Edward Forbes) on p.483.

## 6. Uncertainties

- **Cannot verify**: whether "Cystidea" is a deliberate printed alternate spelling in this paper, or an OCR artifact dropping the æ ligature from running heads (see correctness table).
- **Cannot verify**: the exact wording of the Schlotheim/`aranea` citation line (p.518) — likely missing an OCR-dropped species epithet; existing tree `notes` already flags this appropriately.
- **Cannot verify**: `Von Buch, year 1840` for `hemicosmites`/`pyriformis_buch_1840` against anything printed in this memoir — Forbes gives no year for Buch's establishment of Hemicosmites here at all. He does date Von Buch's main "Über Cystideen" essay to "1845" (p.483, "published at Berlin in 1845"), but `data/sources.yaml`'s own record for that essay (`1844_buch`, "Über Cystideen eingeleitet durch die Entwicklung der Eigenthümlichkeiten von Caryocrinus ornatus") dates it to 1844 (read 3 March 1844), with an existing dataset note that some sources garble the day. This is a genuine citation-year mismatch between what Forbes prints (1845) and the dataset's own bibliographic resolution (1844) — flagged per item A7, not corrected here.
- **Cannot verify**: Forbes' footnote plate citation "pl. 2, fig. 5" (p.520, for the Sowerby Canadian fossil) against `data/trees/1825b_sowerby.g.b.yaml`'s own `illustrations: [{plate: XI, figures: 5}]` for the same figure — the figure number (5) matches but the plate number does not (2 vs. XI). Cannot determine from the text at hand whether this is a Forbes citation error, a journal-internal plate-numbering difference, or a transcription issue in the Sowerby tree file.
- **Cannot verify**: whether `echino-encrinus`/`echinoencrinus`'s `taxa.yaml` authority (`Volborth, 1842`) is reconcilable with Forbes' explicit attribution of the genus to "Von Meyer" (p.504) — would require checking Von Meyer's and Volborth's original publications, out of scope for this text-only audit.
