prints so the owner can decide whether to enter it, rather than checking a

## (b) Astrocystites / Steganoblastus — name, rank, classifying sentences

The paper is not a systematic revision; it has no formal classification list,
diagnosis, or rank hierarchy (no "Class", "Order", "Family" heading appears
anywhere). Its taxonomic content is entirely narrative, concentrated in the
Abstract and a following history paragraph, both on **p. 642**:

- Species name as used in the Abstract: **"Astrocystites (Steganoblastus)
  ottawaensis"** — the current name with the superseded name bracketed after
  it, not marked as a subgenus (no rank word attached to either name).
- The genus-affinity sentence Webby (1968) is referring to when he says
  Hudson (1925) treated *Astrocystites* as a blastoid, quoted in full (p.
  642): *"Astrocystites is seen to be a true blastid and to be a nearer
  Pentremites than is either Asteroblastus, Asterocystis, or
  Blastoidocrinus."* "Blastid" is printed thus; **cannot verify** whether
  this is Hudson's own usage or an OCR misreading of "blastoid" (the rest of
  the scan's OCR is otherwise reliable, and "blastoid" is used correctly
  elsewhere, e.g. "spiracles of the Blastoid type", p. 652).
- This is a comparative/affinity judgment, not a placement in a rank: no word
  "Blastoidea" or "Protoblastoidea" occurs anywhere in the paper (checked by
  full-text search). The word "Blastoid" occurs only as an adjective
  ("Blastoid type", p. 652) or as part of the genus name *Blastoidocrinus*
  (used repeatedly as a comparison genus, e.g. pp. 649, 652, 653, 656).
- Elsewhere the genus is called simply *Astrocystites* throughout the body
  (pp. 643–656); "Steganoblastus" itself occurs only three times total in the
  whole paper: in the Abstract's bracketed species name above, in the history
  paragraph below, and as the title of Bather's 1914 paper in the
  bibliography (item 3, p. 657).
- History paragraph (p. 642, verbatim except bracketed citation-number OCR
  noted below): *"Astrocystites was described in 1896 (9, p. 287). Criticism
  of the generic name, because of similarity to Asterocystis, Haeckel, was
  accepted by Whiteaves and Steganoblastus substituted (10, p. 395) — a name
  suggested by Bather. Original description was republished in 1906 (11, pp.
  316-17) and additional matter given. A more complete study of the cotypes
  was made by Bather (3 [1914], pp. 193-203) and illustrated by six text
  figures and one plate. Bassler, in 1915 (1, p. 83), re-established the name
  Astrocystites."* (The scan renders citation numbers 10 and 11 as "io" and
  "ii" — clear OCR digit errors, resolved here from the bibliography's own
  numbering.)
- One further sentence bearing on classification, p. 656: *"The line of
  evolution in Astrocystites has been distinctly away from the
  Blastoidocrinus type in this particular, and approach to the Pentremites
  type is marked."*

## (c) Nomenclatural acts or new names

None. Every act reported is attributed to someone else: Whiteaves's original
description (1896/1897, per the paper's own inconsistency — see
Uncertainties), Bather's suggestion and Whiteaves's acceptance of
*Steganoblastus* as a replacement name, and Bassler's 1915 re-establishment
of *Astrocystites*. Hudson proposes no new taxon, no synonymy, and no type
designation; he only reports that "Bather designated" the two cotypes as
specimens "A" and "B" (this designation itself is not quoted or located to a
page in the 1925 paper — see the 1927 review, where it is dated to Bather
1914 p. 194).

## (d) Specimens cited

Only "cotypes A and B" are named, borrowed long-term from the Geological
Survey of Canada:

- p. 643: *"Through the kindness of Dr. E. M. Kindle and the Geological
  Survey of Canada we have been allowed a long period for study of cotypes A
  and B."*
- No catalogue number is given anywhere in this paper (no "752" or similar).
- p. 650 uses **"the holotype"** in the singular for one suture study,
  without saying which cotype: *"We have shown these sutures in six
  different areas of the holo-type."* This sits alongside seven other uses of
  "cotype(s)" for the same material (pp. 642, 643, 647, 650, 651) — the paper
  itself is inconsistent between "holotype" and "cotype" language, the same
  kind of contradiction flagged for Fay 1962 (roadmap D9: "Printed type words
  that contradict each other").
- Specimen "B" is individually implicated in structural damage described at
  length on pp. 650–651 (crushing of "1. ant. IR of cotype B", fracture of
  the deltoid).

This paper is itself part of the documentary trail behind Fay 1962's later
note (p. 205) that one of Whiteaves's two syntypes "was lent to Mr. Hudson,
Plattsburg, New York" and "disappeared" when Hudson died: this is the same
George H. Hudson, of the same institution ("State Normal School, Plattsburg,
N.Y.", p. 642), studying the same two cotypes on long-term loan from the
Geological Survey of Canada. The paper does not say which cotype (A or B)
this is, or record any loss — Hudson was alive and the loan evidently
current. This connection may be worth a cross-reference note on the existing
`1962_fay.yaml` specimen entry rather than a new tree.

## (e) Illustrations of the genus

- "Figure I" (p. 644): a photographic enlargement of Bather's 1914 Text-fig.
  3, explicitly reproduced from the earlier work for direct comparison:
  *"This figure is here reproduced from a photographic enlargement and
  becomes our Figure I"* (p. 642).
- Plates I–IV: stereo-photomicrographs of gum-dammar-mounted areas of cotypes
  A and B, described as reproduced "by purely mechanical processes and
  without retouching" (p. 643). Individual plates are cited in the text:
  Plate I (pp. 643–649, the main sutural comparison), Plate II (p. 649,
  radio-deltoid suture "on the left side of the upper figure"), Plate III
  (p. 651, "an inset" reproducing Bather's 1914 Fig. 5), Plate IV (pp. 650–
  651, deltoid structure and crush damage in cotype B).
- No plate or figure depicts a full, undamaged specimen; all are close-up
  photomicrographs of restricted plate areas, explicitly framed as a
  technique comparison against Bather's drawn figure rather than a
  specimen-level illustration.

## (f) Earlier work cited

- **Whiteaves** is never quoted directly; he is cited only in the
  bibliography (items 9–11, p. 657) and in the paraphrased history paragraph
  above (p. 642).
- **Bather 1900** (*Treatise on Zoology*, Part III): one short quoted
  fragment, p. 642: *"BB (5?, sutures not clear)."* (cited "2, p. 210").
- **Bather 1914** ("Steganoblastus", *Geological Magazine*): the most
  heavily cited source, with several block quotations used as targets for
  Hudson's technique comparison, all cited "(3, …)":
  - p. 642–643: *"Except for the tegminals and cover-plates, all the thecal
    elements are closely united, so that it is difficult to distinguish the
    sutures. This difficulty is enhanced by the pitted ornament of the
    surface and by the very deep folding of the plates which form the
    adapical system."* (p. 195 of Bather).
  - p. 643: *"The rhomboidal area above the shoulders of the radials and
    between the grooves is filled with interambulacral plates — their
    outlines are hard to see (Text-fig. 3)."* (p. 196 of Bather).
  - p. 647: a quotation from Bather beginning *"From the top of the
    interradial sutures, the shoulde[r]…"* is given with the right-hand
    portion of every line truncated in the scan (a scanning/extraction
    defect, not a normal OCR misread of individual characters) — **cannot
    verify** the complete wording of this quotation.
  - p. 648 (idx8, continuing onto p.648): further paraphrase of Bather on
    radial upper limits.
  - p. 650: *"The folds between the ridges are very deep, especially in the
    interambulacral area, notably a fold just below the adoral
    interambulacral (Text-fig. 3) …. It may be inferred that the sole effect
    is to endue the theca with quite exceptional rigidity."* (p. 197 of
    Bather).
  - p. 651: *"To judge from the folds, three or four small plates have
    join[ed in] each area, except the posterior, to form a relatively large…
    swollen appearance (IR). This plate rests on the radials and… plates lie
    between it and the grooves."* — again with right-margin loss; **cannot
    verify** the bracketed portions.
  - p. 651–652: *"the tendency of the species is to fuse the originally
    indefinite smaller plates into a few larger plates of definite
    arrangement"* (p. 195 of Bather), disputed by Hudson's own plate count
    (pp. 651–652).
  - p. 652: *"It is clear that the floor-plates and adambulacrals are
    independent morphological elements"* (Bather 1914, p. 199), and *"Whether
    they are really distinct from the floor-plates of the grooves is
    uncertain"* (Bather 1914, p. 196).
  - Hudson uses these quotations adversarially throughout — his purpose is to
    show that Bather's 1914 drawn figure (via Bassler's earlier photograph
    interpretation) contains sutures with "not the faintest trace of
    evidence" in the new stereo-photomicrographs (p. 646), and that
    structures Bather inferred (adambulacrals, p. 652) do not exist.
- **Bassler 1915** cited once for re-establishing *Astrocystites* (p. 642,
  "1, p. 83"), not quoted.
- No source record for Whiteaves 1897, Whiteaves 1906, or Bather 1914 exists
  in `data/sources.yaml`; `1900_bather` is present and reviewed
  (`review_1900_bather.md`).

## (g) Uncertainties

- **Description year for *Astrocystites*.** The running text says
  *"Astrocystites was described in 1896"* (p. 642), but the paper's own
  bibliography item 9 dates the same work — "Description of a New Genus and
  Species of Cystideans from the Trenton Limestone at Ottawa," *Canad. Rec.
  Sci.*, Vol. VII, No. 5 — to "1897", while bibliography item 10, cited for
  the *Steganoblastus* substitution, is "op. cit. 1896" (same title, "1896").
  This is an internal inconsistency in Hudson's own citations (1896 vs. 1897
  for apparently the same volume), not resolvable from this paper alone;
  **cannot verify** which year is correct without the Whiteaves original.
- **"Blastid" vs "blastoid"** (p. 642, Abstract) — see (b) above; **cannot
  verify** which the original prints.
- **Missing word in the Abstract** (p. 642): *"showing the results \[of?\]
  newer methods"* — "of" appears to be dropped; **cannot verify**.
- **Right-margin text loss** on p. 647 (and possibly p. 651) affects several
  consecutive lines of a Bather quotation and Hudson's surrounding
  paraphrase — see (f); **cannot verify** the missing wording.
- **Plate numbering on the unpaginated inserts** (PDF indexes 4, 5, 14) — see
  Page mapping above; **cannot verify** which plate number each insert
  actually carries in print.
- No occurrence/locality statement is given in the running text itself (only
  implicit, via the bibliography title's "Trenton Limestone at Ottawa",
  p. 657).

## Summary for entry decision

The paper adds no new taxon, no formal classification, and no diagnosis. Its
value for this dataset is (1) the affinity statement Webby (1968) cites
(p. 642, quoted in full above), (2) several verbatim Bather 1914 quotations
not otherwise captured in this dataset (no `1914_bather` source record
exists yet), and (3) confirmation that "cotype"/"holotype" usage for this
species was already unstable in 1925, independent of Fay 1962. A tree file
is probably not warranted on its own; the affinity sentence and the
Hudson-loan connection to the lost syntype (see (d)) are better suited to
notes on the existing `astrocystites`/`steganoblastus` taxon records or the
`1962_fay.yaml` specimen entry, at the owner's discretion.
