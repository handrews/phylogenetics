**Nomina nuda versus hedged protologues, plainly stated:**
- *Pentacrinites hamptonii* is a bare name with a figure and no description — the oldest, cleanest nomen-nudum shape in this source.
- *Encrinites lævis* is a bare name (Vanuxem's own, hedged) with a thin physical description (stem diameter, colour) but no diagnosis distinguishing it from any other crinoid — effectively a nomen nudum despite the description, since nothing separates the taxon from its congeners.
- *E. triciclas* has both a figure and a real (if short) diagnostic description ("three different sized crinoid joints, grouped together") — this is closer to an available name than the other three, except that its authorship is explicitly hedged to a different, deceased author ("it appears... Eaton") rather than asserted by Vanuxem himself, and no publication by Eaton naming it has been found.
- *Echinus drydenensis* is not a bare name at all — it carries the fullest description of the four (ambulacral count and structure, spine length, exact size) — but the name itself, and the generic placement it depends on, is explicitly conditional ("Should this fossil belong to..."), which is a hedged protologue (the B24 shape) rather than a true nomen nudum. Calling it "seems to have been a nomen nudum" (the tree's current note) undersells the description but is right about the name's non-availability, since a conditional name of this kind is unavailable regardless of how much text accompanies it.

None of the four has a `taxa.yaml` record except *lævis*, which has one that
appears to be wrong (see above).

## 4. Cases for the data model

**A. YAML location-field defect (data-file issue, confirmed reproducible).**
`data/trees/1842_vanuxem.yaml` lines 34–42, `hamiltonensis_vanuxem_1842`'s
occurrence `location` list, parses (checked directly with `yaml.safe_load`)
to 6 elements, not 7:
```
['upper quarry', 'the hill back of the Seminary', 'West-Hamilton village',
 'Madison County', 'New York', 'United States upper quarry']
```
The final `- United States` entry swallows a stray indented continuation
line (`  upper quarry`) as a folded plain scalar, producing "United States
upper quarry" as one string and duplicating "upper quarry" (already item 0).
Confirmed as a live parse defect, not a formatting quirk of my reading. Not
fixed here, per instructions.

**B. Attribution pattern on `hallii_conrad.t.a_1842` (now located, p.163).**
The printed context is: "List of Fossils from the Reports, etc. of T. A.
Conrad, identified and named to this time, which are found in the Hamilton
Group," followed by items grouped under rank-less headings ("Testacea,"
"Crinoidea," "Crustacea"); under "Crinoidea." stands the single line
"Nucleocrinus hallii." with nothing else. This is B25's case exactly — the
roadmap already cites this passage by name: "Vanuxem 1842 is the older form
of the same thing: a name credited to Conrad with no description, already
recorded through `attributedTo`." What the roadmap entry could not fix
without the fuller read is the page (163) and the precise shape of the
credit: it is not Vanuxem crediting Conrad *for this name specifically*, but
a blanket credit covering an entire per-unit list ("Reports... of Conrad,"
plural, undated — likely Conrad's own antecedent New-York Survey annual
reports, which have no `sources.yaml` record of their own, as distinct from
`1842_conrad.t.a`, a Journal of the Academy of Natural Sciences paper). The
same list-and-blanket-credit shape recurs at the end of the Pentamerus
limestone (p.119, credited to Conrad, no Crinoidea heading, no Lepocrinites
entry) and the Chemung group (p.184, "identified and named by Mr. Conrad up
to this time"). Whether `new: true` on `hallii_conrad.t.a_1842` should stand,
given that the printed line looks like a citation of someone else's
(uncaptured) prior act rather than an act Vanuxem's own text performs, is the
open question the tree's own comment ("a little unclear how to handle this
situation") already flags — this reading round confirms the case is real and
gives it a page, but the question itself is unresolved.

**C. `Encrinites lævis` — taxa.yaml attribution likely wrong.** The genus
and species are proposed by Vanuxem himself, in the first person, hedged
("for the present may be termed") — there is no mention of Conrad on either
page 132 or 133. `data/taxa.yaml`'s `lævis_conrad.t.a_1842: auth:
[conrad.t.a], year: 1842` credits Conrad with no basis found in this source.
Either the record is simply wrong, or it rests on some other 1842 Conrad
publication not checked here (`1842_conrad.t.a`, the Journal of the Academy
paper, pp. 235–280) — but nothing in `data/trees/1842_vanuxem.yaml` currently
carries this species at all, so the record floats unattached to any tree
node or `pages` citation either way. This is not this source's fault to fix,
but it is this source's text that contradicts the record as it stands.

**D. `E. triciclas` — a name attributed, hedged, to a different, deceased
author, inside another author's book.** "This name, it appears, was given to
this species by the late Prof. Eaton" (p.183) is a third distinct shape
alongside B25's Conrad case and A3's `attributedTo`/`in` mechanism: here
Vanuxem himself is uncertain of the attribution he is passing on ("it
appears"), the credited author is dead and left (so far as found) no
publication of the name, and the hedge is about *authorship*, not about the
name's validity or the taxon's placement — none of the C-axis markers
(`provisional`, `questionable`, `tentative`) is quite the right fit, since
those hedge the *taxon*, not the *credit line*. `attributedTo` (A3) captures
the credit; nothing in the current field set captures "and the source itself
doubts its own credit line." No `eaton` author record exists yet in
`data/authors.yaml`.

**E. `Echinus drydenensis` — a fully described, conditionally-named
species.** Unlike the other three names, this one is not a bare name: the
specimen gets a real diagnostic paragraph (ambulacral structure, count,
size, spine length) before the tentative name is offered. The hedge sits
entirely on the generic placement and, through it, on the name itself
("Should this fossil belong to the genus Echinus, its name will be E.
drydenensis"). This is B24's hedged-protologue shape (Billings 1858:
"it is probable... would be appropriate"), not B16's open-nomenclature
axis and not a plain nomen nudum — a conditional name attached to a genus
membership the author has not committed to is a different thing from a name
with no description at all, even though both end up unavailable. The
tree's characterization ("tentatively proposed but seems to have been a
nomen nudum") is right about the outcome but flattens this distinction; worth
keeping the two shapes (bare name vs. hedged-but-described name) apart if a
`Echinus drydenensis` node is ever added.

**F. A recurring printed structure: per-unit systematic lists blanket-
credited to Conrad.** At the end of at least three rock units (Pentamerus
limestone p.119, Hamilton group pp.161–163, Chemung group pp.184–185),
Vanuxem inserts a list headed "identified and named by Mr. Conrad up to this
time" (wording varies slightly), organized under bare rank-less headings
("Testacea, or Shells.", "Crinoidea.", "Crustacea."). This is the same shape
as G7's "tree assembled from several printed structures," but smaller-scale
and repeated: each list is itself a compiled structure with its own implicit
taxonomy-within-a-taxonomy, attributed wholesale to a second person, embedded
in the first person's narrative. `Nucleocrinus hallii` is the only
echinoderm caught in one of these lists; the Pentamerus limestone's list
(p.119) notably does *not* include `Lepocrinites gebhardi`, even though that
species is fully described and figured in the same unit's prose — meaning
the list is not a complete index of the unit's named fossils, just a
distinct printed structure with its own scope.

**G. Rankless heading answers the root's own question.** As covered in §2,
"Crinoidea." is printed (p.163) as one of three bare category words in a
list ("Testacea," "Crinoidea," "Crustacea"), with no rank term attached. This
is G8's territory (rank stated in the tree, not the record) from the other
direction: here the source prints a name with *no* rank at all, and the
tree's root borrows a rank ("Family") from a different source's record
(Miller 1821, per `taxa.yaml`) rather than from anything Vanuxem prints.
Correct as an organizing container (per Ground rules, the root carries no
`pages`/`auth` claiming this is what Vanuxem himself asserts), but worth
flagging since the assignment asked the question directly.

**H. Disarticulated plates at the very bottom of the record (G9).** The
oldest echinoderm material in the volume (p.36, Calciferous group — the
lowest fossiliferous rock in the district) is exactly the case G9 names:
"one of the plates of the head of an encrinite," unnamed, undescribed beyond
a passing comparison. This is the textbook instance of G9's "the earliest
echinoderm records are plates, older than any articulated fossil" — present
here as scope not yet captured, not an error.

## 5. Source record check

Title page (unpaginated leaf, PDF index 8): "GEOLOGY OF NEW-YORK. PART III.
COMPRISING THE SURVEY OF THE THIRD GEOLOGICAL DISTRICT, BY LARDNER VANUXEM.
ALBANY: PRINTED BY W. & A. WHITE & J. VISSCHER. 1842." — matches
`sources.yaml`'s title, year, and sole author (`vanuxem`) exactly (case
only differs). Copyright notice: "Albany, 1842. SAMUEL YOUNG, Secretary of
State." Dedication letter to Governor Seward is dated precisely: "Albany,
January 1, 1842." Running heads throughout read "GEOLOGY OF THE THIRD
DISTRICT," consistent with the title.

Illustration numbering: wood-cuts are numbered sequentially through the
**entire volume**, not per chapter or per rock unit — confirmed independently
by a direct textual cross-reference ("the one figured in wood-cut 51," p.184)
and by the run of numbers actually observed in sequence (9, 12, 14, 16, 25,
31, 32, 38, 41, 44, 45, 48, 49, 50, 51, ... 80). The last wood-cut in the
book, No. 80 (the Hamilton agelacrinite, p.306), is explicitly printed out of
its topical order for a stated production reason: "There being no time
before that part of the Report which relates to the Hamilton group went to
press, to have a wood-cut of the Hamilton agelacrinite prepared, it is
placed here at the conclusion of the entire work" (p.305), matching the
Table of Contents' own forward reference (p.8: "Closing finally with a
wood-cut of the Hamilton agelacrinite, (described at page 158,)").

Everything in `sources.yaml`'s `1842_vanuxem` block checks out against print.

## 6. Uncertainties

- The signature/gathering numbers printed at intervals through the volume
  ("Geol. 3d Dist. 9," "12," "16," etc.) are a separate numbering track from
  the wood-cut numbers; OCR sometimes places a wood-cut's own numeral
  (e.g., "9." at the head of p.65) adjacent to running-head text, and I have
  not independently confirmed from page images that these are typographically
  distinct rather than the same counter — cannot verify without the scanned
  images.
- p.98/idx98's running-head page number OCRs as "80," which is inconsistent
  with the confirmed −9 offset (true printed page 89); almost certainly an
  OCR digit transposition, not a real pagination break — cannot verify
  without the scan, though it is not an echinoderm page and does not affect
  any finding above.
- The Greek etymon behind "Agela-crinites" OCRs as "ctgele" (almost certainly
  ἀγέλη, confirmed by Vanuxem's own gloss "a herd or group") — an OCR
  artifact, not a data question.
- Ligature/spelling variants of "lævis" appear as "Icevis" (p.132) and
  "laevis" (p.133) in the OCR text; both almost certainly render the same
