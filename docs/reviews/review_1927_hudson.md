## (b) Astrocystites / Steganoblastus — name, rank, classifying sentences

As in the 1925 paper, there is no classification list, diagnosis heading, or
rank hierarchy anywhere in the paper (checked by full-text search for
"Blastoidea", "Protoblastoidea", "class", "order", "family" — see below).
All taxonomic content is narrative, concentrated in the Abstract, p. 097:

- Species name as printed in the title and Abstract: **"Astroeystites
  (Steganoblastus) ottawaensis, Whiteaves"** (p. 097) — same bracketed
  synonym-after-current-name convention as the 1925 paper, again with no
  rank word on either name. ("Astroeystites" for "Astrocystites" is almost
  certainly an OCR misreading — the genus is spelled correctly "Astrocystites"
  throughout the running body text, e.g. p. 099 — but the heading/caption
  instances themselves cannot be independently re-checked from this scan.)
- The classification-relevant sentence, quoted in full (p. 097): *"...the
  species shown to be an interesting example of a transition stage between a
  primitive form of papular respiration and the highly specialized
  respiratory mode of the Eublastoidea."* This names a formal higher
  taxon — **Eublastoidea** — but as the endpoint of an evolutionary
  trajectory ("a transition stage… toward"), not as a placement of
  *Astrocystites* inside it. No other occurrence of "Eublastoidea",
  "Blastoidea", or "Protoblastoidea" appears in the paper.
- A hedge about *Astrocystites*'s isolation, p. 097: *"Each species is the
  sole representative of its genus, of its family, and possibly also of its
  sub-order."* No family or sub-order is named anywhere in the paper; both
  "family" and "sub-order" occur exactly once each, in this single sentence
  (checked by full-text search). This sentence also covers *Blastoidocrinus
  carchariaedens*, discussed in the same paragraph as a parallel case of
  "splendid isolation" — it is not exclusively about *Astrocystites*.
- Locality/occurrence statement, p. 097: *"This unique species, from the
  Trenton (Curdsville) of Ottawa, Canada, is known only through three
  specimens."* (See (d) — only two specimens are ever discussed.)
- p. 106, a rhetorical aside rather than a classification: *"Bather
  designated 'specimen A' as the holotype — in specimen B do we have a
  different species?"* — Hudson raises, without resolving, the possibility
  that cotype B represents a distinct species from the (Bather-designated)
  holotype-bearing cotype A, on the basis of the two specimens' very
  different surface-groove ornamentation.

## (c) Nomenclatural acts or new names

None. As in 1925, every act is attributed elsewhere: the species and genus
to Whiteaves, the substitute name *Steganoblastus* implicitly (not restated
here), and the "holotype" designation to Bather ("Bather designated
'specimen A' as the holotype", p. 106; cf. "cotypes of A. ottawaensis which
Bather designated as specimens 'A' and 'B' (1, p. 194)", p. 098, citing
Bather 1914). Hudson himself proposes no new taxon and no synonymy; his own
open question about specimen B possibly being a different species (p. 106)
is explicitly left unresolved, not enacted.

## (d) Specimens cited

- p. 097 (Abstract): *"is known only through three specimens"* — but the
  paper analyses and illustrates only two, consistently called "cotype A"
  and "cotype B" throughout (e.g. pp. 098, 100–108); no third specimen is
  ever named, numbered, or discussed anywhere else in the text. This is a
  direct internal inconsistency in the paper's own material count.
- p. 098: *"Plates VI and VII contain, save for the tegmenal region, a
  complete analysis of the cotypes of A. ottawaensis which Bather designated
  as specimens 'A' and 'B' (1, p. 194)."* — this is the paper's own citation
  locating Bather's A/B designation to Bather 1914, p. 194.
- No catalogue/registration number is given for either cotype anywhere in
  this paper (contrast Fay 1962's "Canadian Geological Survey 752" for the
  same material, which this paper never mentions).
- p. 106 states cotype B "has the longer sutures, is the older specimen, and
  presents the most complex figures" — a comparative age/condition judgment
  between the two cotypes, not a formal role.
- p. 104: *"It was probably this plate that was the cause of the not very
  erroneous representation of a radial with a waved edge which was used by
  Whiteaves in illustration of his species."* — a specific critique locating
  an error in Whiteaves's original figure to a specific plate/specimen
  feature.
- As with the 1925 paper, this text is part of the documentary record behind
  Fay 1962's report (p. 205) that the second syntype "was lent to Mr.
  Hudson, Plattsburg, New York" and was lost when Hudson died — this paper
  and the 1925 paper are Hudson's own published record of that specimen
  while it was in his custody; neither paper records the loan or the loss
  (Hudson was alive), and neither gives a catalogue number that would let a
  reader match "cotype B" here to Fay's specimen "752".

## (e) Illustrations of the genus

Five plates, described as "reproduced from photomicrographs, each of which
were taken through gum-mountings" (p. 098):

- **Plate VI** (p. 101): comparative figures of cotypes A and B side by side,
  labelled by ray position (e.g. "1.ant.IR", "r.post.R", "Y.post.IR");
  caption reads "Astrocystites ottawaensis, Whiteaves".
- **Plate VII** (p. 103): further cotype-A figures with similar ray-position
  labels (a, 1, 2, r, t, etc.).
- **Plate VIII** (p. 105): spine-pit and hydropore detail, figs. 1–3,
  numbered ossicles (used in the anal-area/periproctal-plate discussion,
  p. 100).
- **Plate IX** (p. 107): dendritic-groove figures, figs. 1–2, captioned "To
  be viewed through a stereoscope."
- **Plate X** (p. 109): brachiole-pore/spiracle surface view and a sectioned
  ray, figs. 1–2.
- The text also refers once to a "Plate E" (p. 104: *"In our Plate E the 10
  diameter enlargement of a portion of the radial referred to is viewed
  somewhat edgewise..."*), a letter rather than a roman numeral — **cannot
  verify** whether this is a genuine sixth, lettered plate distinct from
  VI–X, or a captioning/OCR anomaly for one of the numbered plates.
- No plate depicts a complete, undamaged theca; all are close, gum-mounted
  photomicrographs of specific plate groups, continuing the same technique
  described in the 1925 paper.

## (f) Earlier work cited

- **Bather 1914** ("Steganoblastus", cited "(1, p. …)" throughout — the
  paper's own bibliography item 1) is the dominant earlier source, used both
  to attribute the A/B designation (p. 098, "1, p. 194") and to quote and
  dispute specific morphological claims:
  - p. 098: *"For details of the tegmen see Bather's four and eight diameter
    drawings (1, p. 200-201)."*
  - p. 098: *"accepting Bather's interpretation of these features (1, p.
    197)"* — of the spine-pits.
  - p. 100: *"Seven plates converging to a point, where doubtless was the
    vent."* (Bather, p. 196) — disputed immediately: *"The ossicles bearing
    numbers 1, 5, 9, and 10 are not themselves pointed and neither do the
    circumanal plates converge to a point, but they surround a crescentic
    depression still filled with matrix."*
  - p. 100: *"about fifteen other periproctal plates"* (Bather, p. 196,
    surrounding his "seven") — disputed: *"In our plate, however, there are
    31 numbered periproctals."*
  - p. 100: *"This line differs widely from Bather's drawing, both in form
    and position (1, p. 200, and 8, Plate III, inset)"* — of the hydropore
    line.
  - p. 106: *"In some cases it may be that the arrangement of the folds
    indicated rather the original constitution of the young individual (or
    of its ancestor) than any actual separation of plates in the adult"*
    (Bather, p. 195) — negated by Hudson's individual-variation argument in
    the same paragraph.
- **Whiteaves** is credited by name but not quoted directly: as author of
  the species (p. 097 title/Abstract, "Whiteaves"), and criticised once for
  an inaccurate illustration (p. 104, quoted in (d) above).
- **Billings 1859** ("Figures and Descriptions of Canadian Organic Remains,"
  Decade IV, Montreal), cited "(2, p. 19)" for a statement about
  *Blastoidocrinus* (not *Astrocystites*), quoted p. 097: *"In many
  localities they constitute almost the whole of the ingredients of beds of
  solid rock, of from six inches to three feet in thickness."*
- The bibliography (p. 110) is disordered in this scan: item numbers "1."
  through "8." are printed as a block, followed by the titles in sequence
  (Bather 1914, then — after the numbers — Billings 1859, then four Hudson
  papers). Cross-checking against the in-text citation numbers (e.g. "(7)"
  for the stereogram/photomicrograph technique and "(8, Plate III..." for
  the gum-dammar paper, both consistent with the equivalent items in the
  1925 paper's bibliography) confirms items 1, 2, 4–8 are recoverable, but
  **item 3's title is not present in this extraction at all** — a genuine
  gap, not resolvable from the supplied text. It is presumably the source
  for "of which but one nearly perfect and three partially complete thecae
  are known" / "(3, Plates 1-3)" (p. 098), describing *Blastoidocrinus
  carchariaedens* material, but this cannot be confirmed.
- No source record exists in `data/sources.yaml` for Bather 1914, Whiteaves
  1897, or Billings 1859.

## Summary for entry decision

As with the 1925 paper, no new taxon, synonymy, or formal classification is
proposed. The paper's value is (1) an extensive, itemised critique of
Bather's 1914 anal-area and tegmen figures (specific plate/ossicle counts
disputing Bather's numbers — see (f)), (2) the "Eublastoidea" affinity
sentence (b), and (3) the open question about cotype B possibly being a
distinct species (b, (c)) — none of which fit cleanly into a tree node
without capturing disputed counts and hedged rhetorical questions as fact. A
tree file is probably not warranted; the Bather-count disputes and the
"three specimens" discrepancy may be worth a note against the existing
`1962_fay.yaml` specimen entry or the `steganoblastus`/`astrocystites` taxon
records, at the owner's discretion.
