# Hall's Lower Silurian crinoid/edrioasteroid paper: the printings

Three whole-volume scans on hand: the 20th Annual Report, first edition
(1867); the 20th Annual Report, Revised Edition (title page 1868, pasted
note 1870); the 24th Annual Report (1872). Advance sheets of 1866 and of
October 1871, and any separate preprint of the Revised Edition, are not on
hand.

## 1. The printings, as the volumes themselves state them

### 20th Annual Report, first edition (Albany: Van Benthuysen & Sons, 1867)

Ornate title page (p. [i], idx 6): "TWENTIETH ANNUAL REPORT ... TRANSMITTED
TO THE LEGISLATURE APRIL 15, 186[7]. ALBANY: VAN BENTHUYSEN & SONS' PRINTING
HOUSE. 1867."

Assembly-document title (p. 1, idx 8): "State of New York. No. 239. IN
ASSEMBLY, April 15, 1867." Letter of transmittal, same page: "ALBANY, April
15, 1867 ... Sir—I have the honor to transmit the Twentieth Annual Report of
the Regents of the University, on the State Cabinet of Natural History ...
JOHN V. L. PRUYN, Chancellor of the University."

The Regents' own report (p. 6–8, idx 11–13) complains generally of the
printer's delays: "A large portion of the Appendix to the present Report
consists of papers which were communicated with previous Reports, but which
have thus far remained unpublished in consequence of inadequate provision
for the public printing... It is believed that fewer new papers than usual
have been furnished for the present Report, on account of the uncertainty of
their early appearance in print" (p. 7, idx 12).

The table of contents (p. 13–14, idx 18–19) lists, as item under "(K.)
Contributions to Palæontology, by James Hall," item XV: "Descriptions of
some new species of CRINOIDEA and other Fossils from the Lower Silurian
strata, principally of the age of the Hudson-river group" — with no page
number given (the paper is not printed).

Where the paper's place falls in the sequence (p. 304, idx 309), instead of
the text, stands a one-paragraph deferral notice, quoted in full:

> "XV. DESCRIPTIONS OF SOME NEW SPECIES OF CRINOIDEÆ AND OTHER FOSSILS FROM
> THE LOWER SILURIAN STRATA, PRINCIPALLY OF THE AGE OF THE HUDSON-RIVER
> GROUP.
>
> This paper, to the 17th page, was published in November 1866. The
> illustrations occupying the principal part of two plates of figures have
> been made, but could not be engraved in time for the publication of this
> Report. The paper will therefore be deferred in its final publication to
> the next Report. In the mean time, there have been added to the species of
> CRINOIDEA previously described, the following new forms, which are
> likewise figured on one of the plates: Homocrinus constrictus, n.s.; H.
> laxus, n.s.; Poteriocrinus posticus, n.s., and Glyptocrinus parvus."
> (p. 304)

None of the four added names (Homocrinus constrictus, H. laxus,
Poteriocrinus posticus, Glyptocrinus parvus) is described here — no
diagnosis, and the promised plate was never printed in this edition. The
two following papers in the same appendix (Bryozoa/Corals of the Lower
Helderberg, and Bryozoa of the Upper Helderberg/Hamilton groups) and
"Miscellaneous Notices" are likewise deferred to the next Report on the
same page (p. 304). A wholly separate paper a few pages later (item XIX,
Niagara group fossils) carries its own, unrelated advance-print note:
"[Originally printed in advance, for the 18th Report on the New-York State
Cabinet.] ALBANY, December 26, 1864" (p. 305, idx 310) — a different paper,
not to be confused with the target.

None of the target genus/species names (Agelacrinus cincinnatiensis,
Agelacrinus pileus, Cystaster, Streptaster, Lepidodiscus) occurs anywhere
else in this volume. A search for Agelacrinus turns up only "AGELACRINUS
HAMILTONENSIS" (Vanuxem) — a Devonian species in the unrelated "Note on the
Genus Palæaster" paper (pp. 282–299) — never the Lower Silurian species.

### 20th Annual Report, Revised Edition (title page 1868; pasted note: completed 1870)

Two successive scans of the title leaf. The first (idx 8) carries a pasted
correction slip glued over the lower part of the page, reading exactly:

> "NOTE—This Revised Edition was completed in 1870, and the imprint should
> have been so dated, instead of 1868, the date of the First Edition."

The second, clean impression underneath (idx 10) reads: "TWENTIETH ANNUAL
REPORT OF THE REGENTS OF THE UNIVERSITY OF NEW YORK ON THE CONDITION OF THE
STATE CABINET OF NATURAL HISTORY ... REVISED EDITION. PRINTED BY ORDER OF
THE ASSEMBLY. ALBANY: PRINTING HOUSE OF CHARLES VAN BENTHUYSEN & SONS.
1868."

The letter of transmittal (p. 10, idx 19) is reprinted **unchanged**,
still dated "ALBANY, April 15, 1867" and still signed by Pruyn — the
Revised Edition keeps the original 1867 transmittal date even though its
own pasted note says the volume itself was not finished until 1870.

The appendix contents list (pp. 7–8, idx 14–15) runs "L. Contributions to
Palæontology" only through item XII (the Palæaster paper), then jumps
straight to "M. Descriptions of New or Little Known Species of Fossils from
Rocks of the Age of the Niagara Group." **The crinoid/edrioasteroid paper,
the two Bryozoa papers, and the Miscellaneous Notices — items XIII–XVIII of
the first edition — are not listed at all**, and no deferral notice of any
kind appears where the first edition's page-304 notice had stood (the
Palæaster paper runs pp. 330–345, then section M begins directly at p. 347,
idx 362). A search of the whole 560-page scan for Agelacrinus, Cystaster,
Streptaster, Lepidodiscus, cincinnatiensis, vorticellat-, granulatus,
stellatus and pileus turns up only, again, "Agelacrinus hamiltonensis"
(index of the volume, p. 344/idx 456–457) — the unrelated Devonian species.
**The Revised Edition contains none of the paper, and not even an
acknowledgement that it is still owed** — a step backward from the first
edition's one-paragraph notice.

### 24th Annual Report (Albany: The Argus Company, Printers, 1872)

Title page (idx 6): "Twenty-Fourth Annual Report [on] the State Museum of
Natural History ... Transmitted to the Legislature April 18, [187]1 ...
Albany: The Argus Company, Printers. 1872."

Assembly document header and letter (p. 1, idx 8): "State of New York. No.
68. In Assembly. April 18, 1871... To the Hon. Allen C. Beach, President of
the Senate: Sir,—I have the honor to transmit the Twenty-fourth Annual
Report of the Regents of the University, on the State Museum of Natural
History... John V. L. Pruyn."

So the document was **transmitted in 1871** but the volume itself carries
a **1872 imprint**. The volume's own unpaginated closing note (after p.
232, idx 252) resolves this explicitly:

> "The Report on the State Museum of Natural History is printed, in the
> ordinary course, as a legislative document of 800 copies. The copies for
> the Regents of the University and for the State Museum are printed by a
> distinct legislative order, but bear the date of the documentary edition,
> which, in the present instance, was printed and delivered in 1872,
> according to the imprint of the title page."

Contents list (p. iv, idx 16) gives, at last, page numbers for both parts of
the paper:

> "Description of New Species of Crinoidea and other Fossils from Strata of
> the age of the Hudson-river Group and Trenton limestone: By James Hall
> .... 205"
>
> "Description of New Species of Fossils from the Hudson-river Group, in the
> vicinity of Cincinnati, Ohio: By James Hall ... 225"

Immediately before the paper (an unnumbered leaf between printed pages 204
and 205, idx 222), a general editorial note — evidently written for the
whole volume's block of republished papers, not only this one:

> "The following paper, from p. 205 to p. 224 inclusive, is a republication
> of species described and published in 1866, except the first four species
> of crinoidea mentioned in the foot note on page 205, which were first
> published in 1871. All the other species should have been cited as
> published in 1866, but owing to the absence of the author during the
> printing, they are erroneously indicated as n. sp. in the present
> republication."

The paper's own heading and footnote (p. 205, idx 224):

> "DESCRIPTION OF NEW SPECIES OF CRINOIDEA AND OTHER FOSSILS FROM STRATA OF
> THE AGE OF THE HUDSON-RIVER GROUP AND TRENTON LIMESTONE.* By James Hall.
> ... This paper was originally prepared for the twentieth report on the
> State Cabinet. Advance sheets of it were printed and distributed in
> November, 1866, but it not being possible to complete the plates to
> illustrate it in season for the printing of the report, its republication
> has been delayed until the present time. In October, 1871, additional
> copies of the paper were distributed in connection with the paper
> following it in this report, entitled 'New Species of Fossils from the
> Hudson-river Group in the Vicinity of Cincinnati, Ohio,'—the two
> constituting a single pamphlet.
>
> \* This paper was originally published in November, 1866, as advance
> sheets of the Twentieth Report on the New York State Cabinet of Natural
> History. In the present publication, there have been added descriptions
> of Glyptocrinus parvus, Poteriocrinus posticus, Heterocrinus constrictus,
> H. laxus and Proetus parviusculus; of these, figures with their
> explanations were given in a reissue of the paper in October, 1871. There
> have also been added diagrams (1-3) illustrative of the genera
> Glyptocrinus and Glyptaster."

So **there was indeed an October 1871 preprint** — of both papers jointly,
as one pamphlet — confirming the "revised edition" the 1866_hall record's
notes flag as "difficult-to-locate." The paper runs pp. 205–224; the
companion Cincinnati paper pp. 225–232 (footnote, idx 244: "Published
October, 1871, as advance sheets of the State Museum Report. † The paper
appears in the present Report, page 205").

**Errata** (unpaginated, after p. 232, idx 252) touching this paper:

> "Page 205, line 37, dele and Proetus parviusculus.
> Page 214, lines 11 and 29, for PLATE 2 read PLATE 6.
> Page 215, line 15, after AGELACRINUS insert (STREPTASTER)."

The plate-caption text for Plate VI (unpaginated "Explanation of Plates,"
idx 275) is where **Cystaster is actually coined** — not in the main paper
body at all — as a footnote to the caption:

> "HEMICYSTITES (s. g. CYSTASTER*) GRANULATUS Hall, 1871. ...
>
> \* A sac-like body, composed of minute plates and surmounted by five rays
> or ambulacral areas... Although a larger amount of materials would be
> desirable for examination before deciding upon a separation from
> Hemicystites, I would suggest for a provisional term the name of
> Cystaster."

> pileus Hall, 1866" (p. 214); "Agelacrinus [Streptaster] vorticellatus
> Hall, 1866" (p. 215); "Cyclocystoides Salteri Hall, 1866" (p. 218).

Two species are known **only from a plate caption**, with no description
anywhere in either paper's running text: Hemicystites (Cystaster)
granulatus (the Cystaster type) and Pholidops cincinnatensis n. sp. (the
latter's caption gives one sentence of diagnosis: "differing from the P.
subtruncatus in being regularly curved in front... corresponds very nearly
in form with the P. squamiformis of the Niagara group," p. [221 area], idx
278 — this is a case worth flagging on its own, though outside the
brief's named taxa).

Absences worth recording explicitly: no Streptaster diagnosis is printed
anywhere — the name is introduced solely by the one-line errata insertion
quoted above, with no generic description at all (contrast Cystaster, which
does get a diagnostic footnote, however provisional). Lepidodiscus likewise
gets no diagnosis in any of the three volumes — see §3, where this
resolves cleanly rather than being a gap in the data.

## 3. The existing records versus the volumes

**Source records** (`data/sources.yaml`): no tree files exist yet for any of
these sources (`data/trees/` has only `1843_hall.yaml`, `1847_hall.yaml`,
`1852_hall.yaml`), so nothing to check node-by-node; the records themselves
match the volumes closely:

- `1866_hall` (Adv. Pr., pp. 1–17, year 1866) matches the deferral note's "to
  the 17th page" exactly, and its own notes already flag "a revised edition
  with its own difficult-to-locate preprint" — now identified: the joint
  October 1871 pamphlet quoted in §1.
- `1872b_hall` (pp. 205–224 + errata p. 233, year 1872) matches the volume.
  Its note "Possibly should be 1871_hall, proper date a bit unclear" can be
  resolved: **keep 1872b_hall**. The paper's own footnote states most of
  the text is a *republication* of the November 1866 material, explicitly
  now dated 1866 for those species, with only the four (per errata, corrected
  to four, not five) crinoid names and the Cystaster/Glyptaster plate
  material dating to the October 1871 reissue. No single "printing year" for
  the whole record is right; 1872 is right for the record's own printing
  (the physical volume being described), and per-name years belong on the
  taxon records (already mostly done — see below), not the source record.
- `1872c_hall` (pp. 225–232 + errata, year 1872) matches the volume, but
  lacks a `notes` field paralleling `1866_hall`'s: its own footnote states
  "Published October, 1871, as advance sheets of the State Museum Report,"
  i.e. this paper's *first* printing was the October 1871 joint pamphlet,
  exactly as with `1872b_hall`/`1866_hall`. Worth a note to that effect for
  symmetry.
- Neither `1867_hall` nor `1871_hall` exists, and neither is needed: the
  1867 first edition contains no text of the paper (only a notice that
  cites the 1866 advance sheet by name), so it is not a distinct printing of
  the *paper* worth its own record; the October 1871 preprint is not on
  hand and would, if added, be a `printingOf`/sibling record to 1866_hall
  and 1872b_hall/1872c_hall rather than a fresh "1871_hall" — consistent
  with how the existing records already treat this (A11 in
  `docs/semantics-roadmap.md` already reaches the same conclusion: "The
  data has records for 1866 and 1872 only").
- One unrelated but real slip: `1872a_hall` ("Louisville, Kentucky, and the
  Falls of the Ohio") lists only `hall` as author, but the volume's own
  contents page and running head both credit "By James Hall and R. P.
  Whitfield" (p. 181, contents p. iv/idx 16) — Whitfield appears to be
  missing from that record's `authors`. Outside the target paper, but noted
  since it surfaced in the same table of contents.
- The revised edition of the 20th Report is correctly *absent* from
  `sources.yaml` for this paper's purposes — as shown in §1, it contains
  nothing of the paper at all, so no record for it (for this paper) is
  missing.
- `annual-ny` (`data/publications.yaml`) carries only "Annual Report of the
  New York State Museum of Natural History" — the name the institution took
  only from the 21st Report on (per the 24th Report's own transmittal
  letter, which cites the enabling law). The 20th Report's actual title
  reads "on the condition of the State Cabinet of Natural History." A single
  journal record spanning a renaming is ordinary practice, not an error;
  noted only because the two on-hand 20th Report titles print the older
  name.

Three cases worth spelling out for the data model:

- **`cincinnatiensis_hall_1866` is not a nomenclatural act by Hall**: on
  p. 214 the name is explicitly "Rœm.," with Roemer's 1851 citation given in
  full, and Hall never claims authorship. What the record is actually
  standing in for is Bassler's later recognition (1935, quoted in §4) that
  Hall's *figured specimen* (fig. 7, pl. 6) is not truly Roemer's species —
  a misidentification, not a new name. That is a real and useful entity (it
  is what Bassler's `Carneyella cincinnatiensis` is built on), but it is not
  "Hall, 1866" in the ordinary sense of an author coining a name; it needs a
  label along the lines of "Hall's 1866 material identified as
  *cincinnatiensis* Roemer, later shown to be distinct" rather than reading,
  at a glance, as Hall's own species.
- **The Lepidodiscus bracket cannot be original to the November 1866
  advance sheet.** Meek & Worthen's genus Lepidodiscus is dated 1868 —
  two years *after* the November 1866 advance print this text is a
  "republication" of. The bracketed "[Lepidodiscus]" qualifier on
  cincinnatiensis and pileus must therefore have been added when the text
  was reworked for the 1871/1872 republication, silently, alongside the
  genus-group insertions the errata sheet documents for Streptaster. The
  species' own date (1866) and the bracket's necessarily-later date (post
  1868) pull in different directions on the same printed line — exactly the
  kind of thing `editorial`/`notes` exists to record, and exactly why
  `lepidodiscus-subgenus`'s existing note (crediting Meek & Worthen, not
  Hall) is right to keep the two separate.
- **pilea/pileus and vorticellata/vorticellatus**: every occurrence found in
  the three volumes and in Bassler 1935/1936 spells these masculine
  (agreeing with masculine `Agelacrinus`, and with masculine `Streptaster`
  — Greek -aster names are conventionally masculine, cf. Palæaster,
  Petraster). `pilea` is explicable: Bassler's genus Carneyella (into which
  Bassler placed *pileus* as genotype, 1935, quoted in §4) is feminine,
  and a feminine genus would regender the epithet to *pilea* under later
  usage — so keying the canonical taxon on the modern combination's gender
  while keeping `pileus` as the as-printed form is a defensible editorial
  choice, not an error, if that is what happened. `vorticellata` has no
  comparable explanation on the sources at hand: every combination seen
  (Agelacrinus vorticellatus, Agelacrinus [Streptaster] vorticellatus, and
  Bassler's own "Agelacrinus vorticellatus Hall, 1866" and "Streptaster... "
  discussion) keeps the masculine ending, and Streptaster's gender gives no
  reason to regender it feminine. Worth checking against whatever actually
  produced the `vorticellata` spelling; on the evidence gathered here it
  looks like the direction should be reversed (`vorticellatus_hall_1866`
  canonical, `vorticellata` as the variant, if it is attested anywhere).

## 4. The citations

Primary-source confirmation, cross-checked against Bassler 1935 and 1936
(available in this scratchpad) and against what `docs/semantics-roadmap.md`
(A11) and `docs/source-observations.md` already record from Bell 1976 — all
three lines of evidence agree:

| Name | Printing that carries it | Year later authors should cite |
|---|---|---|
| Agelacrinus pileus | Nov. 1866 advance sheet (republished 1872, p. 214) | **1866** |
| Agelacrinus vorticellatus | Nov. 1866 advance sheet (republished 1872, p. 215) | **1866** |
| Agelacrinus (Hemicystites) stellatus | Nov. 1866 advance sheet (republished 1872, p. 215) | **1866** |
| Cystaster (genus) and its type granulatus | Oct. 1871 reissue plate material (printed 1872, unpaginated Plate VI caption) | **1871** |
| Streptaster (genus) | 1872 errata ("insert (STREPTASTER)"), not present in the 1866 or Oct. 1871 material | **1872** |
| Agelacrinus (Lepidodiscus) cincinnatiensis | Roemer, 1851 (Hall's 1866/1872 use is a misidentification, not a new name) | **1851 (Roemer)**, with Hall's material a separate entity (see §3) |

Bassler 1935 (p. 4, this scratchpad) writes, verbatim, "STREPTASTER Hall,
1872" and, separately, "Genotype.—Agelacrinus vorticellatus Hall, 1866" —
i.e. Bassler himself dates the *genus* to 1872 and the *species* to 1866,
which is exactly the split the errata produces. Bassler 1936 repeats
"STREPTASTER Hall, 1872" (twice) and "CYSTASTER Hall, 1871." Bassler 1935
also spells out the cincinnatiensis problem directly:

> "CARNEYELLA CINCINNATIENSIS, n. sp. Agelacrinus (Lepidodiscus)
> cincinnatiensis Hall (not Roemer), Descr. new species fossils Cincinnati,
> Ohio, p. 214, pl. 2, fig. 7, 1871 (advance sheets, p. 6, 1866); 24th Rep.
> New York State Cab. Nat. Hist., p. 214, pl. 6, fig. 7, 1872. This
> well-marked species, erroneously referred to Agelacrinus (now Isorophus)
> cincinnatiensis Roemer, is readily distinguished from that form..."

(Bassler's own citation conflates the two 1871/1872 papers under the
Cincinnati-paper title while citing a page — 214 — that in fact belongs to
the Hudson-river/Trenton paper; understandable, since Hall's own footnote
says the two were "a single pamphlet," but it is Bassler's slip, not the
data's.) `docs/source-observations.md` already reports the matching table
from Bell 1976 (pp. 82, 91): 1866 for pilea, stellatus, vorticellatus
("adv. pub.: 7–8" — the 1866 pamphlet's own pagination); 1871 for Cystaster
and C. granulatus ("adv. pub.: Explanation of pl. 2, fig. 1–6" — note "pl.
2," the pre-errata plate number, matching the errata line "for PLATE 2 read
PLATE 6" exactly); 1872 for Streptaster ("215, pl. 6" — the corrected plate
number, post-errata). All three independent citing traditions (Bell,
Bassler 1935, Bassler 1936) and the primary volume agree completely once the
errata and the republication footnote are read together.

**Bassler's "(Hall), 1856" for stellatus** (1936, p. [4–5], "CINCINNATIDISCUS
(HEMICYSTITES) STELLATUS (Hall), 1856"): this is very likely a simple
typo — a single-digit slip from **1866**, not a different, earlier
citation. Bassler's own earlier paper (1935, same file, "Genotype.—Agelacrinus
(Hemicystites) stellatus Hall, 1866") gives the correct year for the same
species, and the primary text and plate caption in the 24th Report both say
"1866" unambiguously. No earlier printing (1856) of any Hall paper on this
material exists among the three on-hand volumes, the advance-sheet history
recovered above, or Bell's synonymy. Treat as Bassler's own error.

## 5. Uncertainties

- **The revised 20th Report's own "First Edition, 1868" claim is
  unreconciled with the volume on hand.** The pasted note says the imprint
  "should have been so dated, instead of 1868, the date of the First
  Edition" — but the first-edition volume examined here is imprinted 1867,
  not 1868. Either a distinct 1868-imprint reissue of the unrevised text
  exists (not on hand) and is the "First Edition" the note means, or the
  note is using "1868" loosely for the volume we have. Only the missing
  1868 impression (if real) or closer bibliographic checking could settle
  this; marked "cannot verify."
- **Bather's account** (per `docs/semantics-roadmap.md` A11: Geol. Mag.
  1919, p. 73) that the 20th Report was "issued in parts from 1864,
  complete in January 1865 and again in 1867" is not something either
  volume on hand states about itself; the volume's own preliminary matter
  gives no earlier date than 1867. Cannot verify from the volumes on hand;
  only the missing early advance sheets (or Bather's own source) could
  settle whether "complete in January 1865" refers to this Report or to an
  earlier stage confused with it.
- **The precise wording "Plate 2" for the October 1871 pamphlet's
  Cystaster/Glyptaster material** is inferred from the errata line and from
  Bell's citation (both point to the same pre-correction numbering); the
  October 1871 pamphlet itself is not on hand to confirm the plate carried
  exactly that number rather than some other pre-correction numbering.
- **OCR-uncertain readings**, all resolved here from context but flagged:
  "906"/"907"/"Q11"/"99()" for pages 206/207/211/220 in the 24th Report (the
  digit garbling is consistent and the running heads confirm the readings);
  the revised-edition offset near p. 322–345 (roman/arabic confusion at
  several folios, e.g. "(Su) wo wo" for what is almost certainly "323");
  and "Homocrinus" (1867 first edition, p. 304) versus "Heterocrinus" (the
  genus used everywhere else, including the 1872 republication of the same
  four names) for constrictus/laxus — likely simply a generic reassignment
  between 1867 and 1871/72 rather than an OCR error, since both spellings
  are otherwise clean, but noted as "cannot fully rule out OCR" given no
  third occurrence to triangulate against.
- **Pholidops cincinnatensis n. sp.**, known only from its plate caption
  (24th Report, unpaginated, Plate 6/7 area) with one sentence of
  description and no page-range placement in the running text, was not one
  of the brief's named taxa but surfaced during the search; flagged in case
  it matters for future entry of this source.
